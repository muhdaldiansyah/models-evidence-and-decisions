# Decision 0005: Chapter 1 Cold-Transfer Forms

## Status

Accepted (2026-08-15)

## Decision

Chapter 1 uses two parallel unfamiliar-domain cold-transfer forms for the exit production task and delayed retest:

1. **Form A — refrigerated warehouse cooling risk**, a mainly physical/engineering problem.
2. **Form B — emergency temporary-housing allocation after a flood**, an institutional/policy problem.

The full pilot forms live at `chapters/01-decisions-questions/transfer.md`.

The two forms use the same required first-pass outputs and the same eight diagnostic rubric dimensions already specified for Chapter 1. They are selected for pilot and are not treated as empirically calibrated or equivalent until reader testing supports that conclusion.

## Why

The pair is deliberately different in mechanism rather than merely in surface domain.

Form A emphasizes:

- target physical state versus sensor and dashboard records;
- prediction under uncertainty;
- intervention questions about operating changes;
- dynamics and delay;
- constraints and consequences in a mostly non-strategic environment.

Form B emphasizes:

- target population versus observed applicant records;
- prediction versus effect of an allocation action;
- selection and incomplete records;
- positive consequence claims versus normative allocation criteria;
- alternatives and constrained resources;
- adaptive or strategic response to an allocation rule.

Together they test whether the learner can transfer the same Chapter 1 reasoning operations across substantially different problem structures.

## Use in Chapter 1

- For self-study, the learner completes first the form from the domain in which they have less prior familiarity.
- The other form is used for the 7–14 day delayed retest.
- During pilot testing, form order is counterbalanced to separate form difficulty from practice effects.
- Prompt facts must be sufficient for reasoning without requiring refrigeration engineering, food-safety regulation, housing law, or disaster-program expertise.
- Learners must not invent missing domain facts; missing information should be surfaced and assessed for decision relevance.

## Calibration requirement

Before Chapter 1 is frozen:

- test both forms with representative readers;
- compare completion time and dimension-level rubric patterns;
- inspect whether domain familiarity materially drives performance;
- revise or replace a form if hidden domain knowledge or surface complexity dominates the intended reasoning task;
- do not introduce a numerical pass/fail threshold unless pilot evidence supports one.

## Architecture preserved

This decision does not change the Chapter 1 title, central question, core competence, six-section structure, 24-page / 4-hour budget, primary water-supply anchor, pendulum contrast, student-assessment contrast, diagnostic rubric dimensions, transfer target, or book architecture.

## Reopen only if

- pilot evidence shows the two forms are materially incomparable;
- one form requires substantial hidden specialist knowledge despite supplied facts;
- a form systematically tests a different competence from the Chapter 1 transfer target;
- a replacement pair demonstrably improves cross-domain transfer measurement.
