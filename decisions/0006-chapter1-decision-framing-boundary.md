# Decision 0006: Chapter 1 Decision-Framing Boundary

## Status

Accepted (2026-08-16)

## Decision

Chapter 1 teaches decision framing at practical depth without teaching formal decision analysis.

A decision situation should make visible, when material:

- the person, group, or institution with authority or responsibility for the immediate decision;
- the decision, judgment, authorization, or action actually at issue;
- candidate alternatives that can be chosen, authorized, requested, negotiated, or otherwise pursued;
- material consequences for relevant stakeholders or systems;
- values, requirements, obligations, or constraints needed to evaluate those consequences;
- evidence and uncertainty that shape beliefs about what the consequences would be.

These elements interact and may be revised. They are not a mandatory one-directional checklist.

## Analysis, recommendation, and decision remain distinct

An analysis may describe, predict, estimate, or otherwise characterize relevant facts and consequences.
A recommendation advises what should be chosen given stated or assumed evaluative or decision premises.
A decision is the selection, authorization, or commitment made by the relevant decision-maker.

The book must not use `analysis`, `recommendation`, and `decision` as synonyms.

Any older Chapter 1 wording that describes a recommendation as a `decision conclusion` must be read as saying that a recommendation can support a decision, not that the recommendation is itself the decision.
That wording should be synchronized before Chapter 1 is declared stable for drafting.

## Evidence and choice

Evidence and models can improve beliefs about the consequences of alternatives.
They do not by themselves determine which consequences should matter most or which alternative should be chosen.
A recommendation or choice therefore requires both consequence claims and evaluative or decision premises.

This extends Research 03's positive/normative distinction rather than replacing it.

## Alternatives

The initially supplied alternatives must not be treated as automatically complete.
When the option set is materially narrow, Chapter 1 requires the learner to consider at least one plausible missing alternative, which may be a combined action, contingent action, information-gathering step, staged action, delay when feasible, or escalation/authorization route.

Systematic alternative generation and formal value-focused methods remain Chapter 10.

## Authority

An attractive action is not automatically a feasible alternative for the named decision-maker.
If another actor controls the action, the decision situation should identify the required request, authorization, negotiation, escalation, or other governance route rather than silently assuming authority.

The Chapter 1 water-supply anchor and both cold-transfer forms must supply any authority facts needed by the learner; they must not require hidden knowledge of municipal, warehouse, or emergency-housing governance.

## Consequences

A decision-relevant consequence is a material outcome, effect, burden, benefit, cost, risk, or other result that may occur under an alternative.
One analytical target rarely exhausts all decision-relevant consequences.
Consequences must remain distinct from the values or requirements used to evaluate them.

## Chapter boundaries

Chapter 1 owns practical recognition of decision-maker, authority, alternatives, consequences, stakeholders, evaluative premises, constraints, and evidence relevance.

Chapter 10 owns formal value structuring, objectives, measurable attributes or metrics, systematic alternative generation, and trade-off structure.

Chapter 11 owns formal decisions under uncertainty, expected utility, risk attitude, decision trees, formal sensitivity analysis, and value of information.

Chapter 12 owns formal optimization constraints, feasible regions, robustness, regret, scenarios, and adaptive plans.

Chapter 15 owns strategic response, incentives, principal-agent problems, gaming, and endogenous behavioral response.

## Sources

The bounded Research 05 evidence layer promotes:

- `keeney1996valuefocused` for active alternative creation and the warning against treating the initial option set as complete;
- `nasem2026decisionmaking` for the practical separation among facts, values, alternatives, predicted consequences, uncertainty, and decision, while preserving iteration.

The already-promoted `bradley2016structured` remains a supporting applied source.

## Architecture preserved

This decision does not change the book architecture, Chapter 1 title, central question, core competence, six-section structure, 24-page / 4-hour budget, water-supply anchor, pendulum or student-assessment contrasts, cold-transfer forms, rubric policy, or transfer target.

## Reopen only if

- pilot evidence shows that the minimum decision frame creates unacceptable cognitive load;
- the authority distinction cannot be supplied without hidden domain knowledge in the chosen cases;
- Chapter 10 or Chapter 11 formalization reveals a genuine dependency conflict;
- a materially better formulation preserves the same competence with lower burden.

Ordinary prose revision or example refinement is not a reopening condition.
