## Central question

What matters, to whom, and what options exist beyond those offered?

## Core competence

Structure values and consequences, distinguish values from measurable objectives and metrics, identify stakeholders and constraints, and generate alternatives instead of accepting a fixed option set.

## Role in the book

Chapter 10 opens **Part III: Choose**, and it is a change of subject rather than a change of topic.

Parts I and II asked what the evidence supports. From here the book asks what should be done, and the first job is to establish that this is a different kind of question — one that nine chapters of evidential discipline cannot settle, and that a reader who has just acquired that discipline will try to settle with it anyway.

Chapter 9 wrote the opening: it closed by listing four things the utility could not settle from evidence, all of them value questions.

**The source position is unusual and is recorded in full at `../../decisions/0017` clause 1.** The obvious source, `keeney1996valuefocused`, could not be obtained in full, and its source note has prohibited relying on it for procedures since Chapter 1. The framework is taken **as reported at** `bradley2016structured`, obtained in full and read at the relevant pages.

## Examples / recurring cases

### The anchor: the tenth recurrence

For the first time the anchor must produce a **decision** rather than an analysis.

`case-data.md` freezes: the committee paper with its two options and its unusable objective; the seven affected parties and the two consulted; the four stated constraints and what survives inspection; the objectives hierarchy; and seven derived alternatives.

### Deliberately not used

- Any case where the supplied options are adequate.
- Any objective that passes the test as written.
- Personal-life decision examples, which would trivialise the method.

## Exercise architecture

1. **Opening attempt (§1).** What is the utility trying to achieve? Six minutes, unscored.
2. **Test the objective (§3).** Run the two-part format on the paper's stated aim.
3. **Why is this important? (§3).** Take one objective to a fundamental.
4. **The roster (§4).** Who is affected, who was asked.
5. **Constraints (§5).** Sort four, and say who to ask about each.
6. **Generate (§6).** Produce three alternatives not on the paper.
7. **Planted-defect diagnosis (§6).** Five defects.
8. **Cold transfer (§7).** One assigned parallel form.
9. **Retrieval and delayed retest (§7).**

### Planted defects

| Planted defect | Collapse targeted |
|---|---|
| "Our objective is to become the sector leader in resilience" | a vision statement is an objective |
| "The options are Option 1 or Option 2" | the supplied option set is the option set |
| "We consulted the technical experts on what matters" | stakeholders are the people who know things |
| "The budget is fixed, so those are the only feasible options" | constraints are given |
| "Once we have the cost-benefit numbers, the decision follows" | evidence settles what matters |

### Rubric dimensions

1. The stated aim tested against the two-part format.
2. At least one fundamental objective reached by asking why.
3. At least one means objective identified as such.
4. An affected party named who was not consulted.
5. A constraint questioned, with who to ask.
6. At least two alternatives derived from objectives.
7. A conflict between objectives stated without resolving it.

## Transfer target

> Given a paper offering a fixed option set and a stated aim, test the aim against the objective format, produce a small objectives hierarchy distinguishing means from ends, identify who is affected and who was asked, sort the stated constraints, and derive at least two alternatives the paper did not contain.

### Parallel forms

- **Form A — a university deciding on lecture-capture technology** (institutional/technical).
- **Form B — a food manufacturer deciding on an allergen-control upgrade** (industrial).

Both supply: a two-option paper; an aim that fails the format; an affected group not consulted; four constraints of which two dissolve; and at least one objective that is a means mistaken for an end.

Every prior transfer and contrast domain is excluded. Neither domain is sensitive.

Chapter 10 must not claim durable far transfer.
