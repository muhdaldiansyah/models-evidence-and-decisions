# Research 04 — Alternatives, and the Chapter's Own Examples

Cluster R04 of `research-plan.md`. Closed.

Source read directly: `bradley2016structured` printed p. 54. Case material from the Chapter 7 and Chapter 9 files.

## 1. Alternatives come second

> "Decision alternatives describe the different avenues that might be taken to achieve a particular objective. As emphasized in Chapter 2, decision alternatives should be considered only after objectives are understood." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

**Only after.** The ordering is stated as a rule, and it is the whole content of the AFT/VFT reversal made operational.

The report also says where alternatives come from, and it is not the analyst:

> "Seeking stakeholder input to identify and define decision alternatives can be valuable because stakeholders have innovative ideas and a strong local sense of what is threatened, what is creating the threat, what responses are feasible in their community and who or what might be affected by different decisions." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

## 2. How to examine an alternative

> "Each decision alternative can be rated for complexity (which affects the amount of time or cost to implement), effectiveness for the proposed objective, and potential consequences to other objectives." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

**Three dimensions**, and the report unpacks what drives the first: "The scale (how big a project is or might become), the number and level of decision-makers required, the funding, and the acceptability within the community all affect the complexity of a decision" [@bradley2016structured, p. 54].

And a sentence that connects this chapter to the whole of Part II:

> "Effectiveness partially requires factual information; this may require a best estimate of which alternative or combination of alternatives is more likely to achieve the objective." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

**"Partially requires factual information."** Nine chapters of evidential work feed into one of three dimensions of one step. That is not a demotion of Part II; it is a correct placement of it, and the chapter should say so plainly because a reader who has just done nine chapters of evidence will feel the change.

## 3. Two further findings

> "If alternatives are not achieving the objectives, it could imply that they should be reformulated." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

**The set is revisable.** An alternative set that scores badly against every objective is information about the set, not a counsel of despair.

> "Most decisions will affect more than one objective." [@bradley2016structured, p. 54]

With the report's own example: a marine protected area maximises long-term fishery health but "could maximize or minimize tourism, recreation and scientific learning" and "may also detract from economic opportunities" [@bradley2016structured, p. 54].

**This is where trade-offs become visible and where Chapter 10 stops.** Making the conflict explicit is this chapter's job. Resolving it is Chapter 11's, per the generic process table recorded in `research-01` §4.

## 4. The anchor: the committee paper

The chapter opens on a real artefact — a one-page paper of the kind that actually goes to committees.

> **Hillcrest Zone — Capital Options 2027/28**
>
> **Objective:** improve service resilience at Hillcrest.
>
> **Option 1.** Replace the Hillcrest duty pump. Capital cost **£310,000**.
> **Option 2.** Defer to 2028/29 and review.
>
> **Recommendation:** approve Option 1.

**Two options. One stated objective that fails the two-part test.**

"Service resilience" names no quantity that could be more or less; "improve" points a direction at a category. Run p. 50's rule and the paper has no usable objective, which means it has no evaluation criteria, which means the recommendation rests on criteria nobody wrote down.

## 5. Who is affected, and who was asked

| Affected | Asked? |
|---|---|
| The **40 households** at the top of the zone, who lose pressure first | no |
| The other **1,100 households** in the zone | no |
| The **three zones** scheduled for mains renewal from the same envelope | no |
| **Operations crews**, who take the callouts on hot days | no |
| The **regulator**, to whom low-pressure incidents are reported | no |
| The **finance committee** | yes |
| The **operations manager** | yes |

**Two of seven.** And the two who were asked are the two who can supply facts — which is `research-03` §1's point arriving on the anchor: the consultation list was built for the factual question and silently answered the value question.

## 6. Four stated constraints

| Stated | On inspection |
|---|---|
| "The capital envelope is fixed at **£2.4m** for 2027/28." | An annual planning convention. The utility has moved money between years twice in five years. **Soft.** |
| "The pump must be replaced like-for-like because of the housing." | A procurement convention, not a physical limit. A variable-speed drive fits the same housing. **Dissolves.** |
| "Low-pressure incidents must be reported to the regulator." | Statutory. **Real.** |
| "No works between June and September." | Peak demand; the works themselves would cause outages. **Real.** |

**Two of four dissolve or soften.** Both of the soft ones are constraints with authors who could be asked, which is the practical form of p. 7's named trap.

## 7. Objectives, once written

Running the two-part format and the "why is this important" test produces a hierarchy whose top level the utility could agree on in a morning.

**Fundamental:**
- **Minimize** the number of households without adequate pressure during heat events.
- **Minimize** whole-life cost across the network.
- **Minimize** disruption to residents during works.

**Means, and frequently mistaken for ends:**
- Minimize regulatory reportable incidents. *Why is this important?* Because incidents cost money and standing — so it is a means to the first two, and treating it as fundamental is how an organisation ends up managing the reporting rather than the pressure.
- Replace ageing assets. *Why is this important?* Because old assets fail — a means to the first two.

**The second bullet is the chapter's sharpest single demonstration**, because it is the objective the organisation most believes in and it is not one.

## 8. Alternatives the paper did not contain

Derived from the objectives above, not brainstormed.

| | Alternative | Objective it serves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reline the feeder main | pressure, if Mechanism B operates |
| 2 | Fit a **variable-speed drive** to the existing pump | pressure, at lower cost; survives the housing constraint |
| 3 | Create a pressure-managed sub-zone for the top 40 households | pressure, for the households that lose it first |
| 4 | Install local storage at the top of the zone | pressure, without touching the pump |
| 5 | **Upgrade one comparable zone deliberately and instrument it** | pressure later, and evidence now |
| 6 | Targeted contingency for the 40 households on forecast hot days | *adequate pressure* reframed as *water at the tap* |
| 7 | Defer, and instrument Hillcrest through one summer | evidence, at low cost |

**Alternative 5 is the chapter's best moment.**

`../07-targets-identification/chapter.md` §6 recommended arranging one comparison deliberately, as an **identification strategy** — a way to make an identifying assumption true rather than assumed. `../09-evidence-synthesis/chapter.md` §5 repeated it as the thing that would settle the transport question.

Here the same act arrives as a **decision alternative**, derived from objectives with no reference to identification at all.

**Two framings, one act** — and it is the second time the book has shown this shape, after the Chapter 7 positivity / Chapter 9 transport identity. The manuscript should state it once and label it as the book's own observation.

**Alternative 6 is the one that never appears on a capital paper**, because it is not capital. It also reframes the objective: if what matters is households having water on hot days, tankering and direct contact are on the list, and they are cheap.

## 9. Prohibitions for the manuscript

- No weighting, scoring, ranking, or trade-off method — Chapter 11.
- No claim that any alternative is best.
- No recommendation for the utility.
- No claim that any cost figure is typical.
- Keeney's framework cited **as reported at** `bradley2016structured`; nothing attributed to `keeney1996valuefocused` beyond its verified abstract.
- Gregory et al. (2012), Keeney (1992, 2007), Carriger and Benson (2012) cited **as reported**; none was obtained.
- No gloss invented for the six objective properties beyond what the source supports.
- No facilitation or elicitation technique.
