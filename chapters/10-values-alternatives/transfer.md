# Chapter 10 Transfer Design

Status: drafting control. Governs `transfer-form-a.md`, `transfer-form-b.md`, and `transfer-rubric.md`.

Provisional in the same sense as `spec.md`: built on proposed `../../decisions/0017-chapter10-values-terminology-and-boundary.md`.

## Transfer target

Per `spec.md`:

> Given a paper offering a fixed option set and a stated aim, test the aim against the objective format, produce a small objectives hierarchy distinguishing means from ends, identify who is affected and who was asked, sort the stated constraints, and derive at least two alternatives the paper did not contain.

## The changed task shape

Chapter 9 asked for a committee paragraph containing no combined number. Chapter 10 asks the reader to **replace a committee paper**, in six sentences, **with no recommendation**.

The second constraint is the sharper one. Every instinct a competent analyst has points toward telling the committee what to do, and this chapter's whole position is that it has not yet earned the right — trade-offs are Chapter 11's. A response that ends with "Option 2 offers better value" has produced the failure the form is built to catch, without any judgment call by the marker.

## Form design

Both forms supply the same six things, in the same order:

1. **A two-option paper** with a stated aim that fails both halves of the objective test.
2. **Seven affected parties**, of whom the two consulted are both suppliers of facts.
3. **Four stated constraints**, of which two or three are conventions with identifiable authors.
4. **A "fact A"**: an organisational objective, from a plan or a scorecard, which is a means mistaken for an end.
5. **A "fact B"**: an empirical fact that redirects the decision if anyone looks at it.
6. **A format boundary** that makes at least one good alternative structurally inexpressible.

| | Form A | Form B |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Higher education | Food manufacturing |
| Options | renew £480k / migrate £160k + 0.6 FTE | dedicated line £1.2m / retain regime |
| Aim that fails | "enhance the digital learning experience" | "improve allergen safety on Line 3" |
| Affected / asked | 7 / 2 | 7 / 2 |
| Constraints real / soft | 1 / 3 | 2 / 2 |
| Means-as-end (fact A) | "maximize recording coverage" | "zero changeover validation failures" |
| Redirecting fact (fact B) | 68% of playback is eleven modules | no incidents originated on Line 3; two at goods-in |
| Format boundary | a technology-renewal paper cannot propose a non-technology answer | a Line 3 paper cannot propose acting at goods-in |
| Absent party who matters most | 740 disability-service students | consumers with allergies |

Domains, magnitudes, and counts differ so that a reader working both cannot carry an answer across. Every structural feature is matched, with one deliberate asymmetry noted below.

### Deliberate difficulty features

**Fact B redirects the decision and is presented as background.** In both forms it appears last, under "two further facts", in the register of a footnote. A reader who uses it has done the chapter's work; a reader who skips it produces a competent answer to the wrong question.

**The means-as-end comes from a plan.** Fact A is not something a careless person said in a meeting; it is in the strategic plan or on the site scorecard, with an owner and monthly reporting. That is where means-as-ends actually live, and it makes the demotion uncomfortable rather than academic.

**One constraint in each form has no living author.** Form A's union agreement was made with a group that is not the group most affected; Form B's cleaning SOP was written by someone who has left. These are the ones readers accept.

**The absent party who matters most is not the largest group.** 740 students out of 21,000; consumers, who are not in the building. The chapter's §4 makes exactly this point and the forms test whether it landed.

**No recommendation is permitted in the close.** No partial credit for a well-hedged one.

### One asymmetry, recorded

Form A has **three** soft constraints against Form B's **two**. This is deliberate rather than sloppy: Form A's third soft constraint — the three-year retention, where one year is regulated and three is a service description — is the only instance in either form of a constraint that is **partly** real, and it tests a distinction the chapter makes but does not drill.

**The consequence for scoring is that Form A's constraint dimension is slightly harder.** Recorded here so a pilot can check whether the two forms score comparably; if they do not, the retention constraint should move to Form B or be simplified.

### Domain exclusions

Every previously used transfer or contrast domain is excluded: refrigerated warehouse, emergency housing, municipal water, pendulum, student assessment, regional blood supply, city rental assistance, school indoor air quality, hospital emergency department waiting time, city pothole records, food bank client records, household waste recycling centres, clinic appointment reminders, light-van fleet maintenance, social-landlord damp reporting, manufacturing machine guarding, city bus corridors, electricity distribution, charity fundraising, hospital estates, banking risk.

**One judgment recorded.** Form A is set in a university, and Chapter 1's contrast domain was student assessment. The two share the sector and nothing else: that case was about what a mark measures, this one about how a technology-renewal paper constrains an option set. No quantity, actor, or question is shared. Flagged rather than left for a reader to notice.

**One deliberate near-miss.** Form B concerns allergen control, where real harm is at stake. The form takes no position on allergen management, presents no value as typical, and asks only how a decision paper was framed. Judged non-sensitive in the sense `spec.md` uses, and the judgment is recorded rather than assumed.

## What a strong Form A answer should notice

- **The aim fails both halves**, and the consequence: £480k against £160k plus staff time, with nothing on the page able to discriminate.
- **Three passing objectives that choose differently** — students reached, whole-life cost, staff time — with the third favouring Option 1 for a reason the paper never states.
- **Fact A is a means.** *Maximize recording coverage* demotes in two steps to students learning the material.
- **Fact B undercuts fact A.** If 68% of playback is eleven modules, universal coverage is an expensive route to an objective that concentrated coverage serves better.
- **The 740 disability-service students** are the absent party who most changes the decision, and constraint 4 — no downloads — was agreed with a different group entirely.
- **Three of four constraints are negotiable**, and two of the three bear on the objective that matters most.
- **The format boundary**: a technology-renewal paper cannot propose repeat sessions, a note-sharing scheme, or an attendance-policy change.

## What a strong Form B answer should notice

- **The aim fails both halves.**
- **Three passing objectives that choose differently**, with the first — read at site level rather than line level — pointing away from Line 3 entirely.
- **Fact A is a means**, and a revealing one: the site measures changeover validation because it can, on the line where fact B says nothing has gone wrong.
- **Fact B is the redirect.** Two of three incidents originated at goods-in. £1.2m is being proposed for the part of the process with no incident history.
- **Consumers with allergies** are the absent party the whole paper exists for. **Lines 1 and 2** are a strong second, since they share the cleaning crew and changeover window.
- **The 2018 SOP has no living author**, which is why it feels immoveable.
- **The format boundary**: a Line 3 paper cannot propose acting at goods-in.

**Every rubric dimension has a Produce item**, which is the first time since Chapter 8 that the mapping is complete. Chapter 9's `transfer.md` recorded a dimension with no dedicated item; that is not repeated here.

## Pilot notes

Untested. Four things a pilot should measure.

**Time.** 45 minutes for seven items plus a replacement paper. Matches Chapter 9.

**Whether fact B is used.** It is the difference between a competent answer and a correct one, and it is presented as background on purpose. If most readers skip it, the finding belongs in the chapter — §6's derivation moves should include *look at what the record says about where the problem is* — rather than in the form.

**Whether the no-recommendation constraint holds.** Expected to be the most-violated instruction in the book so far.

**Whether the two forms score comparably**, given Form A's third soft constraint. If Form A scores systematically lower on that dimension, the retention constraint should be simplified.
