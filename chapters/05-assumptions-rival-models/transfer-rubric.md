# Chapter 5 — Cold-Transfer Rubric

Open this file only **after** completing the transfer response.

Score each dimension from **0 to 2**. Use the dimensions diagnostically; there is **no validated aggregate cut score**.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adequacy stated | Not addressed | Notes the analysis has a purpose | States what it is built for and what it is **not** built for |
| Stakes assessed | Not addressed | Notes the decision matters | States what happens if the analysis is wrong, and uses that to bound the criticism |
| Size problem | Not found | A number questioned without arithmetic | The division produced, and what it implies stated |
| Limit or extreme | Not found | Asserted the formula "might break down" | The specific behaviour at a limit named, with why it cannot be right |
| Unstated assumption | Not found, or a stated one repeated back | An assumption named | Named, **and** what changes if it is false |
| Rival explanation | Not offered | An alternative reading offered | An alternative reading that would **reverse the recommendation** |
| Discriminating observations | None named | Named for some items | Named for every item, or its unavailability stated explicitly |
| Prioritisation | Item 5 not answered, or all items called equally important | An item chosen | An item chosen **and** the choice defended by what would change |

The last two dimensions are the discriminating ones.

**Every item must carry what would settle it.** A criticism with no discriminating observation is a worry, and worries are what this chapter exists to replace.

**At least one item must be capable of reversing the recommendation.** Criticism that could not change the decision is decoration, however rigorous it looks.

Regardless of any total, major category errors include:

- producing general caveats — *the data may be incomplete, assumptions may not hold, more research is needed* — which would apply unchanged to any analysis in any field;
- criticizing the analysis as poor work rather than as incomplete work;
- rebuilding the analysis instead of criticizing it;
- offering a sensitivity analysis as the criticism;
- proposing "further research" in place of a named observation;
- treating the rival explanation as a preference rather than as something to be excluded;
- inventing a fact the form did not supply;
- concluding that because all models are wrong, an objection cannot be settled.

## A note on tone

Neither analysis in these forms is incompetent. Both are the kind of thing a capable person produces under time pressure, and both reach a conclusion that follows from what they contain.

A response whose main finding is that the author was careless has usually stopped one question early. The interesting failures here are structural: a figure nobody divided, a formula nobody pushed, an assumption nobody wrote down, and a fact read one way when it admits another.
