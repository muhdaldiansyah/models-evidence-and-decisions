## The analysis you have been given

> **Proposal: replace postal appointment reminders with SMS**
> *Prepared for the Clinic Operations Group*
>
> The clinic currently posts a reminder letter before each outpatient appointment. **18%** of appointments are not attended.
>
> **Recommendation: switch to SMS reminders.**
>
> **Volume.** The clinic holds approximately **9,500 outpatient appointments per year** and sends approximately **42,000 reminder letters per year**.
>
> **Cost.** Postage and printing cost **£31,000 per year**. SMS would cost approximately **£2,400 per year**.
>
> **Effect.** Non-attendance is driven by patients forgetting. SMS arrives closer to the appointment and is read sooner. Modelling the reduction as proportional to the share of patients reached by SMS, non-attendance falls from **18%** to **11%** at current SMS coverage, and would reach **0%** at full coverage.
>
> **Conclusion.** The switch saves £28,600 per year and reduces non-attendance by seven percentage points, with further gains available as coverage rises.

## Supplied facts about the clinic

- A mobile telephone number is recorded for **71%** of registered patients.
- Among patients who missed **two or more** appointments in the last year, a mobile number is recorded for **44%**.
- Patients are asked for a mobile number at registration. Registration is completed at a desk in the main entrance.
- The clinic's records show the appointment date, the outcome code, and whether a letter was sent. They do not record why an appointment was missed.
- A staff survey last year listed the reasons patients gave for missing appointments: transport, work or caring commitments, illness on the day, not knowing the appointment had been made, and forgetting.
- The clinic serves a catchment with two hospitals; some patients attend both and receive letters from each.
- Interpreting services are booked separately and are cancelled if a patient does not attend.

## Produce

Write a criticism containing at least the following four items. For **each**, name the observation that would settle it — or say plainly that none is available.

1. **A size problem.** One figure in this analysis does not survive a division. Find it, do the arithmetic, and state what it implies.
2. **A limit or extreme problem.** The analysis states what happens at full coverage. State why that cannot be right.
3. **An unstated assumption.** The conclusion rests on something the analysis asserts but never establishes. Name it, and say what changes if it is false.
4. **A rival explanation that would reverse the recommendation.** Two of the supplied facts, read together, suggest the switch could make non-attendance worse rather than better. Identify them and explain why.

Then answer one further question in a sentence:

5. **Which single one of your four items, if right, would most change what the operations group should do?**
