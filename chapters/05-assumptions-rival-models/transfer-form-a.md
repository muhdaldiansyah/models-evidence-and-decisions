## The analysis you have been given

> **Proposal: close the Western Household Waste Recycling Centre**
> *Prepared for the Environment Committee*
>
> The council operates four household waste recycling centres. Rising operating costs require one to close.
>
> **Recommendation: close Western.**
>
> **Usage.** Western handled **9,400 tonnes** last year, **12%** of the four-site total. The other three handled 21,900, 24,600 and 22,400 tonnes respectively. Western receives approximately **90,000 visits per year**.
>
> **Savings.** Closing Western saves **£240,000 per year** in staffing, maintenance, and site costs.
>
> **Service impact.** Western users will travel to Northgate, on average **4 km** further. Modelled additional queue time at Northgate is **1.2 minutes** per visit, using the standard queue formula *W = q ÷ (c − q)* where *q* is arrivals per hour and *c* is service capacity per hour.
>
> **Conclusion.** Western is the least-used site. Closure delivers £240,000 of savings with negligible service impact and no reduction in recycling tonnage, since displaced users will use Northgate.

## Supplied facts about the sites

- All four sites have **two unloading bays**. A typical visit occupies a bay for about **4 minutes**.
- Northgate, Eastfield, and Southway open **six days a week, 8am to 4pm**.
- **Western opens four days a week, 8am to 4pm.** It has done so since a staffing reduction three years ago.
- Western is the only site serving the two largest housing estates in the district without a river crossing.
- The council does not record where visitors travel from.
- Fly-tipping incidents are recorded by the street cleansing team, in a separate system, and were not consulted for this analysis.

## Produce

Write a criticism containing at least the following four items. For **each**, name the observation that would settle it — or say plainly that none is available.

1. **A size problem.** One figure in this analysis does not survive a division. Find it, do the arithmetic, and state what it implies.
2. **A limit or extreme problem.** The analysis uses a formula. State what it does at a limit or extreme, and why that cannot be right.
3. **An unstated assumption.** The conclusion rests on something the analysis never states. Name it, and say what changes if it is false.
4. **A rival explanation that would reverse the recommendation.** The analysis reads one fact one way. Give a different reading of the same fact that would make Western the *wrong* site to close.

Then answer one further question in a sentence:

5. **Which single one of your four items, if right, would most change what the committee should do?**
