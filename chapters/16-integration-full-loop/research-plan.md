## Standing rules carried forward

## A smaller research base, stated rather than disguised

**Chapter 16 adds no machinery.** Its content is the fifteen chapters already written, and its competence is choosing among them.

**Its external research is therefore lighter than any chapter since Chapter 1, and deliberately so.** Two sources are load-bearing where Chapters 7 to 15 needed three or four each. Padding the list would misrepresent where this chapter's authority comes from, which is the book's own earlier work.

**What the chapter does need from outside** is a warrant for one empirical claim — that people sort unfamiliar problems by surface features until they know enough to sort by structure — and a check on how well that claim has held up.

## What was obtained, and how

**`chi1993classic`** — Michelene T. H. Chi, "Experts' vs. Novices' Knowledge", *Current Contents* "This Week's Citation Classic", CC/NUMBER 42, 18 October 1993. **A single page**, obtained in full and read directly.

It reproduces the 1981 paper's abstract verbatim and adds Chi's own retrospective account of how the finding emerged.

**The 1981 paper itself could not be obtained.** Chi, Feltovich and Glaser, "Categorization and representation of physics problems by experts and novices", *Cognitive Science* 5: 121–152. Three routes attempted: the Wiley full text (403), a university mirror (403), and a Defense Technical Information Center report version, which returned a PDF containing no extractable text.

**Because the document is one page, no page locator is required** — the whole document is the locator. This is not an application of `../../decisions/0022` clause 8's exception; it is a document with no internal pagination to lose.

**`masonsingh2016categorization`** — Andrew Mason and Chandralekha Singh, "Categorization of Mechanics Problems by Students in Large Introductory Physics Courses: A Comparison with the Chi, Feltovich, and Glaser Study", arXiv:1602.08775. Obtained in full and read directly.

**It carries no printed page numbers**, and is cited by named section — Abstract, Introduction, Conclusions. **This is the second application of the bounded exception proposed at `../../decisions/0022` clause 8, and `../../decisions/0023` clause 6 records the dependency explicitly: if the author declines that clause, both applications fall.**

## Two sources already in the bibliography, used at their existing depth

`butler2010transfer` and `schwartz2011contrasting` were promoted for Chapter 1 and verified **against author-provided abstracts**, not read in full. Both bear directly on this chapter's subject.

**Attempts were made to upgrade both and both failed.** Butler returned 401 from the author's site and a connection failure from a second mirror. Schwartz's **author's accepted manuscript** was obtained — sixty-three double-spaced pages with a running head — but **it carries none of the published article's pagination**, and citing an accepted manuscript by section against a bibliography entry that gives published page numbers is precisely the confusion the standing pagination rule exists to prevent.

**So both are used at exactly the depth Chapter 1 used them, with Chapter 1's cautions intact, and nothing further is claimed.** The Schwartz manuscript is recorded as obtained and declined.

## Bounds — what was deliberately not researched

**No expertise literature beyond the categorization finding.** No deliberate practice, no chunking, no ten-thousand-hour claims, no expert-performance research.

**No analogical-transfer literature.** Gick and Holyoak were sought once and not obtained; nothing is claimed about analogical transfer.

**No instructional-design literature beyond what Chapter 1 already promoted.** Cognitive load, scaffolding, and worked-example research are `../../decisions/0008`'s territory and are not reopened.

**No artificial-intelligence literature at all.** `README.md` states that AI in this book "is an application and stress test, not a separate intellectual foundation", so the chapter's AI case is worked entirely with the book's existing chapters and no AI-specific source is sought or cited. **This is a deliberate consequence of the governed block and is recorded so that its absence does not read as an oversight.**

**No machine-learning evaluation methodology.** Held-out accuracy appears in the case as a fact about what the system's builders reported, not as a technique the chapter teaches.
