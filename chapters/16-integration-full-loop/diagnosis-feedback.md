# Chapter 16 — Diagnosis Feedback

**Open this only after you have written all five diagnoses.**

Four of these statements contain a defect. **One of them routes the problem correctly and then does nothing with it**, which is a different failure and the one a reader of this chapter is most likely to commit.

Nothing here is scored.

---

## 1. "We worked through all fifteen chapters on this and none of them was skipped."

**The defect.** Fifteen applied stages is not a finding about thoroughness; it is usually a finding about the absence of judgment.

This book's own specification names "mechanically applying every stage to every problem" as one of its major category errors, and assigns the remedy here: **require relevance justification, and allow explicit "not material here" findings.**

**What it stops you concluding.** Which parts mattered. A document in which every stage was worked gives its reader no way to tell the four rows that decided the answer from the eleven that were filled in.

**And it costs more than time.** Section 6's charity problem needs four chapters. Working the other eleven would have produced eleven paragraphs of correct, irrelevant analysis, and the person reading it would have to find the four themselves.

**The repair.** Produce a routing record, mark the negatives, **and put a reason on every negative row.** The reasons are the work. Anybody can list the chapters they used.

**A test.** Could somebody disagree with one of your negative rows and say why? If not, you have not written a reason — you have written a label.

---

## 2. "We routed it and found that Chapters 3, 4, 8, and 15 were material. That's the analysis."

**This is the one that routes correctly and stops.**

The routing is fine. On several problems in this book those four would be exactly right.

**The defect is treating the routing record as the output.** It is where the work starts.

**What it stops you concluding.** Anything. A routing record says which questions are worth asking. It does not answer them, and a document containing only the record has established that somebody thought about the problem for twenty minutes.

**And it has a specific consequence in this chapter.** Both of §5's backward revisions were **triggered by working a stage**, not by routing it. The label's identity surfaced while somebody worked the identification question. The payment structure surfaced while somebody worked the strategic question.

**A routing record produced and not used cannot revise itself**, because nothing in it will ever turn up a fact.

**The repair.** Work the material stages far enough to reach a finding, and expect the record to change while you do. **If your routing record at the end of the analysis is identical to the one at the start, either the problem was very simple or you stopped early.**

---

## 3. "The tool predicts a scheduler's code rather than urgency, which means the earlier analysis was wrong."

**The defect.** It converts a backward revision into an accusation, and that is how revisions stop being recorded.

**Nothing about the earlier analysis was wrong.** Chapter 1's question was answered as *how urgent is this repair*, which is what the tool is called, what the field is named, and what everybody in the building says. Chapter 3's answer followed from Chapter 1's.

**What changed is what the question turned out to be**, and nothing available at the framing stage would have revealed it. The label's identity became visible only when somebody asked what the target quantity was, which is a question that arrives seven chapters later.

**What it stops you concluding.** It stops the revision being written down. A team that treats revisions as errors will absorb them into the final document — the conclusion appears, the route disappears — and the next person to work on the system inherits a clean narrative with no map of where the soft ground is.

**The repair.** Record the revision in five parts: what was revised, what triggered it, what the previous answer was, what the revised answer is, and **whether the previous answer was reasonable at the time.**

**The fifth line is the one that matters**, and it is the one that gets left out.

---

## 4. "The problem is a machine-learning system, so we need machine-learning evaluation methods."

**The defect.** It treats an automated system as a new kind of problem rather than an ordinary problem with an automated component.

**Look at what the routing record for §3's tool actually contained.** Fourteen rows, every one of them an ordinary chapter of this book. No row was needed for the tool being automated.

**What the tool automated was a scheduling judgment**, and every question that raises — what the label is, what the score stands for, why the training records exist, whether the relationship still holds, who benefits from the drift — is a question this book asks about judgments made by people.

**What it stops you concluding.** It sends the analysis to a specialist vocabulary that will produce accuracy figures, and accuracy figures were the thing that concealed the problem. **The board minute recorded the 84% precisely because it was the number that answered the question somebody knew how to ask.**

**The repair.** Route it like anything else. **Then, if a stage turns out to need machine-learning methods, you will know which stage and why** — and you will be commissioning specialist work rather than substituting it for the analysis.

**One honest caveat.** There are questions about automated systems this book does not equip you for, and the chapter says so. What it claims is narrower: **the triage is the same triage**, and skipping to specialist methods skips the part that tells you which specialist you need.

---

## 5. "We ran the routing at the start and worked the material stages in order."

**The defect.** *In order* — meaning the book's order — is the waterfall this chapter exists to take apart.

This book's specification names "treating the teaching order as a real-world waterfall" as a major category error, with the remedy assigned to Chapters 16 and 17: a **worked demonstration of two distinct backward revisions.**

**The order is a property of the problem.** Section 4's case started at Chapter 4, because the odd thing about it was a number in the training data. Section 6's case also started at Chapter 4, for a completely different reason. The water case started at Chapter 1 because Chapter 1 was the chapter.

**What it stops you concluding.** It costs time rather than truth, usually — but it also makes revision less likely. **A team working in the taught order has an investment in each stage being finished before the next begins**, and reopening Chapter 1 after Chapter 7 feels like going backwards rather than like ordinary work.

**The repair.** Start where the problem is odd. **Expect to move.** And record the order you actually worked in, because it is information: the sequence in which things became visible is part of what a later reader needs.

---

## What the five have in common

**Two of them are about producing too much** — fifteen applied stages, and the book's order applied whether or not it fits. **Two are about producing too little** — a routing record used as an output, and a revision suppressed because it reads as an error. **One is about producing the wrong kind of thing.**

**And all five describe a document that would pass review.**

Fifteen stages looks thorough. A routing record looks disciplined. Acknowledging that an earlier analysis was wrong looks honest. Bringing in specialist methods looks rigorous. Working in order looks organised.

**That is the difficulty this chapter is about.** Nothing in triage produces a visible artefact of judgment, and the two things that would — a reason on a negative row, and a recorded revision with its fifth line — are exactly the two things a busy person drops.
