## Problem A — the repairs triage tool

**This meets the requirement in `README.md`'s Chapter 16 block: "at least one substantial automated or AI system case."**

### 1. The situation

A local authority manages **14,000 homes**. Tenants report repairs by phone and web form; a contractor attends.

**Since January 2023** an automated tool has scored each incoming report **0 to 100** for urgency. **Reports scoring above 70 are routed as emergencies.**

### 2. What the tool was built on

| Year | Repair jobs in the training set |
|---|---:|
| 2018 | 18,900 |
| 2019 | 19,400 |
| 2020 | **15,600** |
| 2021 | 20,800 |
| 2022 | 21,700 |
| **Total** | **96,400** |

**2020 is 20% below 2019.** Fewer repairs were reported and attended during the pandemic; the number of homes needing repair did not fall.

**The label the tool was trained to predict is the priority code a human scheduler assigned to the job.**

### 3. What the vendor reported

| | |
|---|---:|
| Agreement with held-out scheduler codes | **84%** |
| Agreement with an independent re-inspection of 300 jobs | **61%** |

**These are two numbers about different things**, and the authority's board minute records only the first.

### 4. After deployment

| Quarter | Share of reports scored above 70 |
|---|---:|
| 2023 Q1 | 11.2% |
| 2023 Q2 | 12.4% |
| 2023 Q3 | 14.9% |
| 2023 Q4 | 17.1% |
| 2024 Q1 | 19.8% |
| 2024 Q2 | **22.3%** |

**The share has roughly doubled in six quarters** — a rise of 11.1 points, or 99%.

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jobs completed as emergencies | 4,180 | 4,790 | **5,100** |
| Statutory hazard referrals | 612 | 604 | **631** |

**Emergency jobs are up 22%. Statutory hazard referrals are up 3.1%.**

The referrals are generated by a separate inspection regime with no connection to the tool, and are the nearest thing available to an independent indicator of how many homes are actually in a dangerous state.

### 5. The payment structure

**The contractor is paid £148 for an emergency job and £62 for a routine one.**

**920 extra emergency jobs in 2024 against 2022, at £86 of difference each, is £79,120.**

### 6. The routing record

| Chapter | Material? | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Decisions and questions | **yes** | The decision is whether to keep routing with the tool, not whether the tool is accurate |
| 2 Representation | **yes** | The tool sees a free-text report and six fields; it does not see the property |
| 3 Measurement | **yes** | What does a score of 70 stand for? |
| 4 Observation process | **yes** | The labels are scheduler decisions; the 2020 volume is a pandemic artefact |
| 5 Assumptions and rival models | **yes** | There is a rival explanation for the rise in emergencies |
| 6 Probability and simulation | **no** | Nothing is being forecast, updated, or scored as a probability |
| 7 Targets and identification | **yes** | Does a high score cause a faster repair, or does the scheduler's judgment cause both? |
| 8 Estimation and uncertainty | **yes** | 84% and 61% are two numbers about different things |
| 9 Combining and transporting | **no** | One source of evidence; nothing to combine and nowhere to transport it to |
| 10 Values and alternatives | **yes** | Whose objective — the tenant's, the authority's, or the contractor's? |
| 11 Decisions under uncertainty | **yes** | Is a larger re-inspection worth commissioning? |
| 12 Optimisation and robustness | **no** | No binding envelope, no indivisible schemes, no portfolio |
| 13 Dynamics and feedback | **yes** | The emergency queue is a stock with a fixed servicing capacity |
| 14 Sequential decisions and control | **yes** | This is a policy applied thousands of times, and the state it needs is not observable |
| 15 Strategic interaction | **yes** | The contractor is paid more for emergencies and the score share is rising |
| 17 Deployment and monitoring | *deferred* | Chapter 17's |

**Eleven of fourteen material.** The manuscript must say that **this is unusual**, and that it is why this case was chosen as the substantial one.

### 7. The two backward revisions

**Revision 1 — triggered at Chapter 7, returns to Chapters 1 and 3.**

The identification question asks what the target quantity is. **The tool predicts a scheduler's priority code, not whether a repair is urgent.**

**Trigger: the 84% and the 61%.** Agreement with a human decision and agreement with a physical assessment are not two measurements of one thing.

**What it revises:** Chapter 1's question had been answered as *how urgent is this repair*; it is *how would a scheduler have coded this*. Chapter 3's had been answered as *urgency*. Both were answered reasonably and both were wrong.

**A revision about what the target was.**

**Revision 2 — triggered at Chapter 15, returns to Chapter 8.**

The strategic question surfaces the payment differential and the rising share of high scores.

**What it revises:** any uncertainty estimate computed on post-deployment data describes a process that was changing while it was being measured. The interval is not wrong; it is about a period rather than about a system.

**A revision about an answer having gone stale.**

**The two are different in kind, and the manuscript says so.**

## Problem B — the appeal timing

**Deliberately thin. Four of fifteen chapters material.**

### 8. The situation

A charity mails an annual fundraising appeal. It is deciding whether to move it from October to November.

| Year | Month | Names mailed | Response rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2016 | Oct | 62,400 | 3.4% |
| 2017 | Oct | 64,100 | 3.1% |
| 2018 | Oct | 66,800 | 3.6% |
| 2019 | **Nov** | 71,200 | **3.9%** |
| 2020 | Oct | 69,500 | 4.2% |
| 2021 | Oct | 72,900 | 3.3% |
| 2022 | **Nov** | 78,400 | **4.1%** |
| 2023 | Oct | 81,600 | 3.5% |

| | |
|---|---:|
| October: years | **6** |
| October: mean response | **3.52%** |
| October: standard deviation | **0.34** |
| November: years | **2** |
| November: mean response | **4.00%** |
| Difference | **+0.48 points, or 13.7%** |

**The two November mailings happened because of production delays, not by design.** They are not a random sample of years. **They are also the second and fourth largest lists of the eight.**

### 9. The decision

Next year's list is **90,000 names**. The mean gift is **£27.40**.

| At | Gifts | Value |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3.52% | 3,168 | **£86,800** |
| 4.00% | 3,600 | **£98,600** |

**The difference is worth about £11,800.**

**A split test on next year's mailing costs £4,800 in extra production — about 40% of the value of the difference it would resolve.**

### 10. The routing record

| Chapter | Material? | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Decisions and questions | **yes** | The decision is which month to mail, which is narrower than how to raise more money |
| 2 Representation | no | No model is being built |
| 3 Measurement | no | Response rate is well defined and directly counted |
| 4 Observation process | **yes** | **Why do the two November years exist?** Production delays, not design |
| 5 Assumptions and rival models | no | No model to criticise |
| 6 Probability and simulation | no | Nothing being represented or scored as a probability |
| 7 Targets and identification | no | **A randomised split test is available** — the assumptions Chapter 7 exists to interrogate are satisfied by design |
| 8 Estimation and uncertainty | **yes** | Two observations against a six-year spread of 0.34 |
| 9 Combining and transporting | no | One source |
| 10 Values and alternatives | no | The objective is agreed and uncontested |
| 11 Decisions under uncertainty | **yes** | Is the £4,800 split test worth running? |
| 12 Optimisation and robustness | no | No portfolio, no envelope |
| 13 Dynamics and feedback | no | No stock, no delay that matters at this horizon |
| 14 Sequential decisions and control | no | Annual, but not a policy in Chapter 14's sense |
| 15 Strategic interaction | no | Donors do not know the mailing date is being varied |

**Four of fifteen, and the analysis is complete.**

**Chapter 7's row is the interesting one.** It is not material *because* a randomised test is available — which is precisely the situation Chapter 7 identified as the easy case.

## 11. What these cases may not be used for

- Any claim about a real local authority, housing provider, contractor, charity, or automated system. **Both cases are synthetic.**
- Any claim about how often automated triage tools behave this way. **No frequency is claimed.**
- Any claim about artificial intelligence as a subject. Per `README.md`, AI here is an application and a stress test.
- Any suggestion that eleven of fourteen is the normal result of a triage. The manuscript states that it is not.
- Reworking or re-estimating anything in the water case.
