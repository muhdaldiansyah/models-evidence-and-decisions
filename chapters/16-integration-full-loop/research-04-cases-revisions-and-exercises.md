## 1. Why the water anchor is not the case

**Chapter 16 is the first chapter since Chapter 1 whose worked case is not the water utility**, and the reason is structural rather than stylistic.

The chapter's competence is triage of an **unfamiliar** problem. A reader who has spent fifteen chapters inside one municipal water system cannot demonstrate triage on it: they know what it needs, and they know because they were told, chapter by chapter.

**The water case is referred to throughout and reworked nowhere.**

## 3. Problem A — the repairs triage tool

A local authority manages **14,000 homes**. Tenants report repairs by phone and web form; a contractor attends. Since January 2023 an automated tool has scored each incoming report **0 to 100** for urgency, and reports scoring above **70** are routed as emergencies.

### 3.1 What the tool was built on

| Year | Repair jobs in the training set |
|---|---:|
| 2018 | 18,900 |
| 2019 | 19,400 |
| 2020 | **15,600** |
| 2021 | 20,800 |
| 2022 | 21,700 |
| **Total** | **96,400** |

**The 2020 figure is 20% below 2019**, for reasons that have nothing to do with how many repairs were needed.

**The label the tool was trained to predict is the priority code a human scheduler assigned to the job.**

### 3.2 What the vendor reported

**Agreement with held-out scheduler codes: 84%.**

**Agreement with an independent re-inspection of 300 jobs: 61%.**

### 3.3 What happened after deployment

| Quarter | Share of reports scored above 70 |
|---|---:|
| 2023 Q1 | 11.2% |
| 2023 Q2 | 12.4% |
| 2023 Q3 | 14.9% |
| 2023 Q4 | 17.1% |
| 2024 Q1 | 19.8% |
| 2024 Q2 | **22.3%** |

**The share has roughly doubled in six quarters.**

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Jobs completed as emergencies | 4,180 | 4,790 | **5,100** |
| Statutory hazard referrals | 612 | 604 | **631** |

**Emergency jobs up 22%. Statutory hazard referrals — an independent indicator, generated by a separate inspection regime — up 3.1%.**

### 3.4 The payment structure

**The contractor is paid £148 for an emergency job and £62 for a routine one.**

**920 extra emergency jobs in 2024 against 2022, at £86 of difference each, is £79,120.**

## 5. The two backward revisions

`../01-decisions-questions/spec.md` L582 requires a "worked demonstration of two distinct backward revisions" from Chapters 16 and 17. Both are placed here, both are evidence-triggered, and **they are distinct in kind**.

### Revision 1 — triggered at Chapter 7, returns to Chapters 1 and 3

Working the identification question, the reader asks what the target quantity is — and finds that the tool predicts **a scheduler's priority code**, not whether a repair was urgent.

**The evidence that triggers it is the pair of accuracy figures.** 84% against scheduler codes and 61% against re-inspection is not two measurements of one thing; it is one measurement of agreement with a human decision and another of agreement with a physical assessment.

**What it revises.** Chapter 1's question — what is being asked — had been answered as *how urgent is this repair*. It is actually *how would a scheduler have coded this*. And Chapter 3's question — what does the number stand for — had been answered as *urgency*. **Both were answered reasonably and both were wrong**, and nothing before the identification stage would have revealed it.

**This is a revision about what the target was.**

### Revision 2 — triggered at Chapter 15, returns to Chapter 8

Working the strategic question, the reader finds the payment differential and the rising share of high scores.

**What it revises.** Any uncertainty estimate computed on post-deployment data describes a process that was changing while it was being measured. **The interval is not wrong; it is about a period rather than about a system.**

**This is a revision about the data-generating process changing.**

**The two are different in kind and the manuscript says so**: one found that a question had been misread, the other that an answer had gone stale.

## 6. Problem B — the deliberately thin one

A charity mails an annual appeal. It is deciding whether to move it from October to November.

| Year | Month | Names mailed | Response rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 2016 | Oct | 62,400 | 3.4% |
| 2017 | Oct | 64,100 | 3.1% |
| 2018 | Oct | 66,800 | 3.6% |
| 2019 | **Nov** | 71,200 | **3.9%** |
| 2020 | Oct | 69,500 | 4.2% |
| 2021 | Oct | 72,900 | 3.3% |
| 2022 | **Nov** | 78,400 | **4.1%** |
| 2023 | Oct | 81,600 | 3.5% |

**October: six years, mean 3.52%, standard deviation 0.34.**
**November: two years, mean 4.00%.**
**Difference: +0.48 points, or 13.7%.**

**And the two November mailings happened because of production delays, not by design** — so they are not a random sample of years. **They are also the second and fourth largest lists of the eight.**

Next year's list is **90,000 names**; the mean gift is **£27.40**. The difference between the two rates is worth about **£11,800**. A split test on next year's mailing costs **£4,800** in extra production — **about 40% of the value of the difference it would resolve.**

### The routing record for Problem B

**Material: four.** Chapter 1 (what decision, for what use), Chapter 4 (why do the two November years exist), Chapter 8 (two observations against a six-year spread), Chapter 11 (is the split test worth running).

**Not material: eleven**, each with a reason. Chapter 7 is the interesting one: **a randomised split test is available**, which is precisely the situation in which Chapter 7's machinery is not needed — the assumptions it exists to interrogate are satisfied by design.

**Four of fifteen, and the analysis is complete.** That is the chapter's second main result.

## 7. Exercise design notes

**The opening task is the Chapter 1 comparison and must not be scored.** `../01-decisions-questions/spec.md` records the exit task as "diagnostic rather than a major book transfer gate" with "no validated numerical cut score". Introducing a score fifteen chapters later would change retrospectively what the preserved artifact was.

**The transfer forms must each contain two problems** — one automated system and one thin decision — so that both halves of the discrimination are tested, and tested twice.

**The predicted failure is completeness.** A reader who routes every chapter to every problem has produced a document that looks thorough and demonstrates nothing. **The forms must therefore ask for the negatives explicitly and with reasons**, because a "not material" finding without a reason is indistinguishable from an omission.

**The second predicted failure is triage from the problem statement alone.** Both of the chapter's backward revisions are triggered by things found during the work, and the forms should include at least one fact that changes the routing once noticed.

**The diagnosis task should include one statement that routes correctly and stops** — completing the triage without doing anything with it — because that is the failure a reader of this chapter is most likely to commit.
