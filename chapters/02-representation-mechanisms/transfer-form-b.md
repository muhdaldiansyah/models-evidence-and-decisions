Without consulting the Chapter 2 chapter text, the water case, the checklist, or the rubric, produce **two representations** of the system below — one for each stated purpose — and answer the four questions that follow.

A labelled box-and-arrow sketch plus short notes is the expected form. No calculation beyond arithmetic is needed, and every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something seems missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **40 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## Form B — City rental-assistance programme

### Situation

A city runs a rental-assistance programme. Households at risk of eviction apply; caseworkers review applications; approved households receive a one-off payment made directly to the landlord.

Two different decisions are on the table this quarter.

- **Purpose 1.** The finance committee must be told whether the quarter's budget is sufficient for the applications expected.
- **Purpose 2.** The programme director must say **which households are most likely to be evicted while waiting**, in order to decide whether to change how applications are prioritised.

### Supplied facts

- Budget for the quarter: **$2,400,000**.
- Average award: **$2,000** per approved household. The budget therefore supports about **1,200 awards** in the quarter.
- Applications arrive at about **400 per month**, so roughly **1,200 applications** are expected in the quarter.
- Budget is allocated to five districts by a formula based on district **population**, set several years ago. District 3 receives **25%** of the budget.
- District 3 currently produces **45%** of applications.
- A pending application carries over from month to month until it is decided. At the start of the quarter, **310 applications** were already pending.
- Caseworkers can complete about **380 reviews per month**.
- An award prevents eviction only if the landlord accepts the payment and halts proceedings. The case supplies that most do, and that some do not.
- Eviction proceedings, once filed, typically conclude in **6 to 10 weeks**. The case does not supply how long any individual household has been waiting.
- A community organisation in District 3 actively helps residents complete applications. No comparable organisation operates in the other districts.
- Records show the district, the household size, and the award amount for each application. They do not record whether an eviction was ultimately avoided.

### Produce

1. **Two representations.** Build one for Purpose 1 and one for Purpose 2. For each, draw a boundary, list the parts with what each does, and say which things must be carried forward.
2. **Boundary defence.** For each representation, name one thing you put inside and one thing you deliberately left outside, and give a reason for each in terms of that purpose.
3. **A mechanism, with its status.** Name one phenomenon in this system and draw a mechanism for it. Then state in one sentence what drawing it has and has not established.
4. **The flip.** Name one specific simplification that is **defensible under Purpose 1 and indefensible under Purpose 2**. Write one sentence defending it, and one condemning it.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form A at all.**
