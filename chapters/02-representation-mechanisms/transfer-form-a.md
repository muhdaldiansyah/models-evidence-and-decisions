# Chapter 2 Cold-Transfer Task — Form A

Status: reader-delivery copy. Governed by `spec.md` (Transfer target) and `transfer.md`.

Without consulting the Chapter 2 chapter text, the water case, the checklist, or the rubric, produce **two representations** of the system below — one for each stated purpose — and answer the four questions that follow.

A labelled box-and-arrow sketch plus short notes is the expected form. No calculation beyond arithmetic is needed, and every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something seems missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **40 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## Form A — Regional blood supply

### Situation

A regional blood service collects, tests, stores, and issues red-cell units to six hospitals.

Two different decisions are on the table this week.

- **Purpose 1.** The service must tell the regional health board whether the region will run short of red cells in the next seven days.
- **Purpose 2.** A severe storm is forecast. If collections stop for several days, the service must say **which patients would be affected first**.

### Supplied facts

- Total red-cell stock across the service: **900 units**.
- Total issue to hospitals: **150 units per day**.
- Red cells have a shelf life of **35 days** from donation. Units are issued oldest first.
- Stock and issue by group, in the four groups this case tracks:

| Group | Units in stock | Issued per day |
|---|---:|---:|
| O-negative | 72 | 24 |
| O-positive | 310 | 51 |
| A (both) | 380 | 57 |
| B and AB (both) | 138 | 18 |
| **Total** | **900** | **150** |

- O-negative can be given safely to a patient of any group. The case supplies that hospitals therefore use it in emergencies **before a patient's own group is known**, which is why its share of issues is larger than its share of stock.
- O-negative donors are also less common in the donor population than O-positive donors.
- Collection normally replaces what is issued. A storm closing collection centres would stop replacement but not issue.
- Every donated unit must be tested before it can be issued. Testing takes **24 hours** and is done at one regional laboratory.
- Each hospital also holds a small stock of its own. The case does not supply the size of those holdings.
- Hospitals report issues daily. Reports are by group and by hospital, not by individual patient.
- No strategic actor is assumed. Hospitals may change ordering behaviour if warned of a shortage, but the system is otherwise mainly logistical.

### Produce

1. **Two representations.** Build one for Purpose 1 and one for Purpose 2. For each, draw a boundary, list the parts with what each does, and say which things must be carried forward.
2. **Boundary defence.** For each representation, name one thing you put inside and one thing you deliberately left outside, and give a reason for each in terms of that purpose.
3. **A mechanism, with its status.** Name one phenomenon in this system and draw a mechanism for it. Then state in one sentence what drawing it has and has not established.
4. **The flip.** Name one specific simplification that is **defensible under Purpose 1 and indefensible under Purpose 2**. Write one sentence defending it, and one condemning it.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form B at all.**
