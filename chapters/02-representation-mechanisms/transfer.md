# Chapter 2 Transfer Design

Status: governed design source for the Chapter 2 cold-transfer forms.

**Contains answer commentary for both transfer forms. Do not read this file if you intend to complete the Chapter 2 transfer exercises**; use the reader copies `transfer-form-a.md` and `transfer-form-b.md`, which the manuscript links at the right moment.

Where a reader copy conflicts with this file, this file controls.

## Transfer target

> Given an unfamiliar system and **two different stated purposes**, construct two defensible representations, state what each includes and excludes and why, and identify one simplification that is acceptable under the first purpose and unacceptable under the second.

The final clause is the discriminating one. Two competent representations with no identified flip is the characteristic near-miss.

## Form design

Two parallel forms, one mainly physical/logistical and one institutional, following the pattern governed by `../../decisions/0005` for Chapter 1.

| | Form A | Form B |
|---|---|---|
| Domain | Regional blood supply | City rental-assistance programme |
| Character | Physical / logistical | Institutional |
| Aggregate that looks adequate | 900 units against 150/day — six days | $2.4M supporting 1,200 awards against 1,200 expected applications |
| Disaggregation that breaks it | O-negative: 72 units against 24/day — three days | District 3: 25% of budget, 45% of applications |
| Natural state | Units in stock, by group and by age | Pending applications carried month to month |
| Boundary question | Are hospital-held stocks inside? the testing laboratory? donors? | Are landlords inside? caseworker capacity? |
| Rival mechanisms | Emergency use before typing, versus scarcer O-negative donors | Rents rose fastest in District 3, versus outreach raising the application rate |

Both demand identical structural outputs. Only the surface domain differs, which is what makes them usable as parallel forms and delayed retest.

### Domain exclusions

No Chapter 1 transfer domain may be reused. Refrigerated warehouse, emergency housing, municipal water, pendulum, and student assessment are all excluded, so a reader completing both chapters meets no repeated domain.

## What a strong Form A answer should notice

- The aggregate view (900 ÷ 150 = 6 days) is **correct** and adequate for Purpose 1. It should not be marked as an error.
- O-negative at 72 ÷ 24 = **3 days** is the disaggregation that matters, and the reader must produce it rather than assert that types differ.
- **Shelf life makes age part of the state.** A representation carrying only "units in stock" cannot say what expires when. Strong answers notice that units have an age as well as a group, and that oldest-first issue means the two interact.
- The testing delay of 24 hours means new collections are not immediately available — a boundary decision with real consequences under Purpose 2.
- Hospital-held stocks are **deliberately unquantified** in the form. Naming this as missing information that could change the answer is a strong move; inventing a number for it is a category error.
- Two mechanisms are drawable for disproportionate O-negative depletion — emergency use before typing, and scarcer donors. They point at different responses: change issuing protocol, or change recruitment. Neither is established.
- The flip: treating red cells as one interchangeable pool is defensible under Purpose 1 and indefensible under Purpose 2.

## What a strong Form B answer should notice

- The aggregate view (1,200 awards against 1,200 applications) is **correct** and adequate for Purpose 1.
- District 3 at 45% of applications against 25% of budget is the disaggregation that matters.
- **The backlog is the state.** 310 pending applications carry forward, and caseworker capacity of 380 per month against 400 arrivals per month means the backlog grows. Strong answers notice that the queue, not the budget, may be the binding constraint under Purpose 2.
- Landlord acceptance sits at the boundary. An award that does not stop proceedings does not prevent eviction, so a representation excluding landlords cannot answer Purpose 2 fully.
- **The outreach fact is the trap and the prize.** District 3's application rate may reflect a community organisation's help rather than greater need. A reader who treats applications as a measure of need has aggregated two different things. This is the form's hardest and most valuable observation.
- Records do not show whether eviction was avoided — so the representation contains no outcome, which limits any claim about what the programme achieves.
- The flip: treating applications as interchangeable and need as uniform across districts is defensible under Purpose 1 and indefensible under Purpose 2.

## Parallelism check

Both forms supply an aggregate that is genuinely adequate for the first purpose. This is a design requirement, not an accident: if the aggregate were simply wrong, the forms would test "spot the error" rather than purpose-relative adequacy.

Both forms contain exactly one accumulating quantity that serves as a natural state, one boundary question with a decision consequence, and two rival drawable mechanisms.

## Pilot notes

- Target duration **40 minutes**, a design parameter pending pilot evidence.
- Form order counterbalanced (A→B and B→A), per `../../decisions/0008`.
- Record prior familiarity with blood services and with housing programmes before the first task.
- The delayed form must not be previewed.
- Neither form has been checked by a subject-matter expert. Both use only supplied facts and require no specialist knowledge, but the plausibility of the operating stories is unverified. See `spec.md`, open question 9.
