# Chapter 2 — Cold-Transfer Rubric

Open this file only **after** completing the transfer response.

Score each dimension from **0 to 2**. Use the dimensions diagnostically; there is **no validated aggregate cut score**.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Purposes restated as topics, or the two treated as one | Purposes distinguished but not used to constrain content | Each purpose is stated precisely enough that it visibly decides what goes in |
| Boundary | No boundary, or a boundary drawn at a physical edge with no reason | A boundary is present; inclusion or exclusion defended, not both | Explicit boundary, with one inclusion and one exclusion each defended by the purpose |
| Parts and roles | Parts listed without roles, or an undifferentiated blob | Parts and some roles identified | Parts listed with what each does and a role assigned to each |
| State | No account of what is carried forward, or "everything that changes" | Something carried forward is identified but not justified | What must be carried forward is identified and justified; at least one candidate correctly rejected |
| Grain | Grain not addressed, or defaulted silently | A grain is chosen | Grain chosen and its consequences for what is answerable are stated |
| Mechanism | A mechanism asserted as established, or none drawn | A mechanism drawn; epistemic status vague | A mechanism drawn for a **named phenomenon**, with its status stated correctly and defensibly |
| Simplifications | Simplifications not identified, or all treated alike | Simplifications identified | Omission distinguished from asserted falsehood, with the appropriate defence for each |
| The flip | No simplification identified, or one that is simply an error | A difference between the two representations is named | A specific simplification is named that is genuinely defensible under one purpose and indefensible under the other, with both sentences written |

The last dimension is the discriminating one. Two competent representations with no identified flip is the characteristic near-miss for this chapter.

Regardless of any total, major category errors include:

- producing one representation where two purposes were given;
- writing that something causes something, on the strength of a drawing;
- treating an aggregate as adequate without asking what decision it is being read against;
- calling a quantity `state` when it is recomputable from others already listed;
- defending a simplification as "more realistic" or "more detailed" rather than by reference to a purpose;
- treating the second representation as a correction of the first;
- inventing domain facts the form did not supply.

## Post-task self-explanation

Before editing your original response, answer:

Name a change in reasoning, not merely an omitted detail. Then revise only after preserving the original response for comparison.
