## Form A — the leakage programme

### 1. The baselines

**Overnight-flow rise: 3.1, 2.4, 4.6, 1.9, 3.8, 2.7, 4.1.**
**Mean 3.23. Standard deviation 0.90. Maximum 4.6.**

**Highest quarterly burst count: 1, 2, 0, 4, 1, 2, 1.**
**Mean 1.57. Standard deviation 1.18. Maximum 4.**

Any consistent measure of spread is fine. The range alone would do for item 2.

### 2. Trigger or timer

**The flow limb is a trigger.** Six per cent sits **3.07 standard deviations** above the baseline mean and was **never reached in seven years**. A value past it is genuinely unusual.

**The burst limb is a timer.** Three sits **1.21 standard deviations** above the mean and was **exceeded once in seven years** — a rate of one in seven per year, which over a fifteen-year horizon is **2.1 expected firings from ordinary variation alone.**

**And a strong answer says why the burst limb is a timer**, which is not the same as saying that it is. **Burst counts are small integers with a long tail.** A threshold of three on a series whose mean is 1.57 is barely above the second-commonest value, and small-count series produce occasional high values as a matter of course.

**Do not credit** an answer that calls both limbs badly set. One of them is well set, and saying so is half the item.

### 3. What the 2025 firing establishes

**Four burst events in a quarter equals the baseline maximum.** It sits 2.06 standard deviations above the baseline mean and **the company has already had a year like it.**

**What it establishes:** the threshold was crossed.

**What it does not establish:** that anything changed. A limb that fires on a one-in-seven-year value produces firings at roughly that rate whether or not the network is deteriorating.

**So the board's decision not to refer was, on the arithmetic, defensible** — and **nobody in the room could have known that**, because nobody had the baseline.

**A strong answer says both halves.** An answer that says only the first has excused the board; an answer that says only the second has convicted them.

### 4. The limb that never fired

**Exceeded in 2024 at 6.8% and in 2026 at 6.3%, never in consecutive years.**

**That is not a bug.** The two-consecutive-years condition buys protection against acting on a single bad year, and it was written deliberately.

**What it costs is exactly what happened**: a limb that is exceeded in half the operating years and never fires.

**The right answer names it as a cost that was chosen**, and then asks whether the choice still looks right after four years — which is a different question and the one worth putting to the board.

### 5. The rewritten clause

Two things a good rewrite does.

**Removes the ambiguity.** Something like: *These are two independent conditions. Either one, on its own, requires referral.* Report each limb's status separately, each ending in a verdict.

**And regrounds the thresholds.** The burst limb needs a value outside the baseline range — five, or a count sustained across two consecutive quarters, which is a different and often better construction because it uses the tail's shape rather than its height.

**Credit any answer that notes the cost of raising it**: a threshold far enough out never to fire on ordinary variation also fires late on real deterioration. **There is no setting that avoids both**, and saying so is a stronger answer than a number.

### 6. What the indicators cannot see

**All three are reasonable.** Coverage, process, and outcome — the shape a competent monitoring designer produces.

**And none can see a demand model built on the wrong data**, because a controller working correctly against a wrong target produces exactly these readings.

**A failure that would leave all three acceptable:** any error in what the target *should* be. The controller meets its target, so pressure-against-target improves; the areas are all covered, so coverage is complete; and customers do not contact you about pressure that is technically adequate.

**Note especially that pressure-against-target improved *because of* the problem.** The controller is holding pressure tightly to a target that is too high, and holding it tightly is what that indicator measures.

**An answer that cites the improvement as reassurance has read the pack the way the board read it.**

### 7. The ratio

**6.79 in 2022. 8.14 in 2024. Up 19.9%.**

Urgent repairs raised by the control room are rising much faster than independent pipe-failure reports.

**Why nobody reported it:** one number belongs to the control room, the other to street works, and **the ratio is nobody's report.**

**Credit an answer that notes this is a structural feature of how organisations divide work**, not a failure of attention.

### 8. Diagnosis by stage

**The failure entered at Chapter 4** — the observation process. The demand model was built on five years of consumption data, two of them pandemic years in which non-household consumption fell by roughly a third, in exactly these areas.

**The symptom appears at Chapter 13** — night flow rising while a controller keeps meeting its target.

**Nine stages, and four years.**

**And no monitoring arrangement could have caught it**, which is the item's real content. Adding a fourth output indicator would have detected nothing.

### 9. The revision trigger, in two directions

**World-side** — something like: *overnight flow above the baseline maximum in any two consecutive quarters.*

**Model-side** — and this is the one usually omitted: *if the demand model is more than three years old, or if any year in its training window is later identified as atypical, the pressure target is re-derived.*

**The model-side condition is what would have caught this**, and neither the original clause nor most answers will contain one.

## Form B — the attendance plan

### 1. The baselines

**Persistent-absence change: +1.8, −0.6, +2.3, +0.4, +1.1, +2.9, −0.2.**
**Mean +1.10. Standard deviation 1.21. Maximum +2.9.**

**Safeguarding referrals: 8, 11, 6, 13, 9, 7, 10.**
**Mean 9.14. Standard deviation 2.23. Maximum 13.**

### 2. Trigger or timer

**The absence limb is a trigger.** Four points sits **2.40 standard deviations** above the mean, never reached in seven years.

**The referral limb is a timer.** Twelve sits **1.28 standard deviations** above the mean and was exceeded once in seven years — **2.1 expected firings over fifteen years.**

**And the reason is worth naming:** referral counts vary with staffing, with reporting practice, and with which member of staff is on duty, as much as with need.

### 3. What the 2025 firing establishes

**Fourteen exceeds the baseline maximum of thirteen by one.** That is slightly stronger evidence than Form A's and still not much.

**Defensible not to refer, and nobody could have said so.**

### 4. The limb that never fired

**Exceeded in 2024 at +4.7 and 2026 at +4.3, never consecutively.** A chosen cost, not a bug.

### 5. The rewritten clause

As Form A: state that the limbs are independent, report each separately with a verdict, and reground the referral threshold outside the baseline range — **or replace it with a rate rather than a count**, which is a better construction where the denominator has been growing.

### 6. What the indicators cannot see

**Contact within five days improved from 88% to 94%** — and it improved because more pupils were flagged and caseworkers contacted the easier cases first.

**More pupils contacted is not more pupils helped**, and no output indicator distinguishes them.

### 7. The ratio

**14.42 in 2022. 18.20 in 2024. Up 26.2%.**

More pupils flagged per statutory order sought.

### 8. Diagnosis by stage

**Entered at Chapter 4**: the model predicts *which pupils a head of year referred to pastoral support* — a staff decision, not a risk — and it learned that on four years of which two had attendance recording that changed twice.

**Symptom several stages later**, in a caseload that grew without the outcome moving.

### 9. The two-direction trigger

**Model-side:** *if any year in the training window is later reclassified as atypical, or if the recording standard changes, the scoring model is re-derived.*

**That condition would have fired in 2022**, before the plan started.

### 10. The observation this form is built to allow

**Safeguarding referrals are not an independent indicator here.**

The flag routes pupils to pastoral caseworkers. **Caseworkers are the people who make safeguarding referrals.** So flagging more pupils mechanically raises the referral count, which is the very series the revision clause uses as its check.

**Two consequences, and a strong answer draws both.**

**The timer analysis is weakened**, because the baseline was generated by a system that did not contain the flag. The seven baseline years describe a different process from the four operating years.

**And the check is partly self-referential.** A rule that watches a number its own operation inflates will fire on its own activity — which is a Chapter 15 shape arriving inside a Chapter 17 instrument.

**This is the hardest observation either form allows and it is not required for a good answer.**

## The trap in both forms

**Both forms hand you a rule that was carefully written.**

An observable quantity. A numeric threshold. A named owner. A stated frequency. A reporting obligation "whether or not either has triggered." **It has everything Chapter 12 said a signpost needs.**

**And one of its two limbs was a timer**, which four lines of arithmetic on data the organisation already held would have shown before the plan was signed.

**The rule was not sloppy. It was ungrounded**, and those look identical on the page.

## Four answers that look right and are not

**"The threshold fired, so the trigger worked."**
It fired on a value the organisation had already seen. Firing is not evidence.

**"The board ignored a fired trigger, so this is a governance failure."**
The board reached the defensible answer. It reached it by misreading a two-line rule, which is a different problem and is not fixed by escalation.

**"One indicator improved, so the intervention is doing something."**
It improved because of the failure.

**"We need better monitoring."**
For the failure in these forms, no monitoring would have helped. **That is the defect the diagnosis task singled out**, and it is the one that feels most like a solution.

## A note on tone

**Nobody in either form does anything wrong.**

Two organisations wrote revision clauses — which is more than most do — appointed owners, reported annually whether or not anything triggered, and monitored coverage, process, and outcome monthly.

**The failure is that a threshold was never checked against a baseline, and that a two-line rule was read quickly by people who already knew what the other line said.**

**Form B concerns children's attendance**, and the form describes no pupil and takes no position on any attendance or safeguarding practice. Every routing in it sends a pupil toward more support. The subject is a monitoring arrangement.
