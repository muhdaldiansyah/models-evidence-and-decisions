## Case 1 — operating Chapter 12's signposts

### 1. The plan, quoted from Chapter 12 unchanged

> **Watch.** Peak-week demand against the Chapter 1 forecast, reported each September. Heat events per year, already counted for the regulator.
>
> **If.** Peak-week demand exceeds the forecast by more than four per cent in two consecutive summers, **or** heat events exceed six in a single year, stage 2 of the trunk reinforcement enters the following year's programme at an assumed **£1,150k**.
>
> **Owner.** The asset planning lead reports both signposts to the capital committee each October, whether or not either has triggered.

Chapter 12 also wrote, in its own text: **"Four per cent, two consecutive summers, six heat events — those numbers are arguable, and being arguable is the property that matters."**

### 2. The baseline nobody computed

**Peak-week demand against the Chapter 1 forecast, the seven years before the plan:**

| 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +0.4% | −1.2% | +2.1% | −0.8% | +1.6% | +3.3% | −0.5% |

| | |
|---|---:|
| Mean | **+0.70%** |
| Standard deviation | **1.55** |
| Maximum | **+3.3%** |
| The 4% threshold, in standard deviations above the mean | **2.12** |
| Years in which 4% was reached | **0 of 7** |

**Heat events per year, the same seven years:**

| 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 5 | 2 | **7** | 4 | 3 | 5 |

| | |
|---|---:|
| Mean | **4.14** |
| Standard deviation | **1.55** |
| Maximum | **7** |
| Years exceeding six | **1 of 7** |
| Implied rate | **1 in 7 per year** |
| Expected firings over the plan's 15-year horizon, from baseline variation alone | **2.1** |

**One limb was a trigger. The other was a timer.**

The demand limb sits above everything the baseline produced. The heat-events limb fires on a value that had already occurred once in seven years.

**Both figures come from data the utility already held**, and the calculation is four lines.

### 3. Four years of operation

| Year | Peak-week demand vs forecast | Heat events | What the October report recorded |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2023 | +1.8% | 4 | Neither signpost triggered |
| 2024 | **+5.2%** | 5 | Demand exceeded four per cent; not two consecutive summers; no trigger |
| 2025 | +2.9% | **7** | **Heat events exceeded six** |
| 2026 | **+4.6%** | 5 | Demand exceeded four per cent; not consecutive with 2024; no trigger |

### 4. The two findings

**The demand limb never fired, and Chapter 12 foresaw exactly this.** Its own text: "If demand jumps in a single year rather than two, stage 1 will be inadequate and the trigger will not have fired." **Demand exceeded four per cent in two of four years and never in consecutive ones.** The rule behaved as written.

**The heat-events limb fired in 2025 and nothing happened.**

The October 2025 report recorded the value. **The committee minute records "signposts reported; no action required."** The plan says stage 2 "enters the following year's programme". It did not.

**Why not.** The report presented both signposts together; the demand limb — which everybody had been watching for two years — had not fired; and **the disjunction in the rule was read as a conjunction.** The rule said **or**. The report was read as **and**.

### 5. And the arithmetic complicates the verdict

**The 2025 value of seven equals the baseline maximum** and sits **1.84 standard deviations** above the baseline mean.

**So the firing is not strong evidence that anything changed.** A limb that fires on a one-in-seven-year value produces firings at roughly that rate whether or not the climate is moving.

**The committee's decision not to act was, by accident, defensible.** Nobody at the meeting could have said so, because nobody had the baseline.

**A right answer reached by reading "or" as "and" is not a right answer**, and the manuscript must not let it become one.

## Case 2 — monitoring Chapter 16's repairs tool

All facts about the tool are frozen in `../16-integration-full-loop/case-data.md` and are not restated here. **The monitoring arrangements below are new to this chapter and add no new fact about the tool.**

### 6. What the authority monitored

| Indicator | 2022 | 2024 | Read as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly job volume | 1,180–1,290 | 1,180–1,290 | stable |
| Repairs completed within target time | 94.1% | **95.6%** | **improved** |
| Tenant satisfaction | 81% | 82% | flat |

**All three were reported monthly to the housing committee. All three looked fine or better.**

**And the completion figure improved for a reason:** routing more jobs as emergencies gets them attended sooner, which is what that target measures.

### 7. What nobody reported

| | 2022 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Emergency jobs | 4,180 | 5,100 | +22% |
| Statutory hazard referrals | 612 | 631 | +3.1% |
| **Ratio** | **6.83** | **8.08** | **+18.3%** |

**Both numbers were collected.** Emergency jobs by the repairs team; statutory hazard referrals by the environmental health team.

**Neither team reported the ratio, because the ratio is nobody's report** — the same finding Chapter 15 reached about a different pair of numbers, and the manuscript notes the repetition once.

### 8. Diagnosis by stage

| | |
|---|---|
| Where the symptom appeared | **Chapter 13** — an emergency queue whose inflow doubled against a fixed servicing capacity |
| Where the failure entered | **Chapter 4** — the tool was trained on a label that is a scheduler's decision |
| Distance | **nine stages and eighteen months** |
| Could any monitoring arrangement have caught it? | **No** |

**A tool that reproduces its label produces outputs that look right by construction.** The monitoring was not inadequate. It was watching outputs, and the failure was not in the outputs.

### 9. Which stages monitoring can and cannot see

| Failure enters at | Would monitoring show it? |
|---|---|
| 1 — the question | **No.** Everything downstream answers the wrong question competently |
| 3 — what a number stands for | **No** |
| 4 — why the records exist | **No.** Outputs match the label by construction |
| 7 — identification | **No.** Predictions can be fine |
| 8 — estimation | Sometimes, and late |
| 13 — dynamics | **Yes.** Waiting times move |
| 15 — strategic response | **Yes, if the right ratio is watched** |

**The early stages are invisible and the late ones visible**, which is the reverse of where attention usually goes.

## 10. What these cases may not be used for

- Any claim about a real utility, local authority, contractor, committee, or automated system. **Both cases are synthetic.**
- Any new physical fact about the water network. Case 1 adds none.
- Any new fact about the repairs tool beyond Chapter 16's frozen data plus the monitoring arrangements in §6.
- Any claim about how often signposts are misread, or about how often deployed tools fail this way. **No frequency is claimed.**
- Any suggestion that the committee or the asset planning lead behaved badly. The rule was misread, and the misreading produced the defensible answer for the wrong reason.
- Any climate claim. The heat-event series is synthetic and the chapter draws no conclusion about whether heat events are becoming more frequent — which is, in fact, one of the things the case shows the utility cannot tell from its own data.
