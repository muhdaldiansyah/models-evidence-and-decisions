## The situation

A **multi-academy school trust** of nine secondary schools committed in 2022 to a three-year attendance-improvement plan. The plan introduced an automated **absence-risk flag**: a weekly score for every pupil, with pupils above a threshold routed to a pastoral caseworker.

**The plan carried a written revision clause**, agreed by the trust board:

> **Watch.** The trust-wide persistent-absence rate, expressed as the change in percentage points on the previous year, reported each September. Safeguarding referrals to the local authority, already counted for statutory returns.
>
> **If.** The persistent-absence rate rises by more than **four percentage points** in two consecutive years, **or** safeguarding referrals exceed **twelve in a single year**, the flag's scoring model is referred for re-specification at an assumed cost of **£95,000**.
>
> **Owner.** The director of inclusion reports both figures to the trust board each October, whether or not either has triggered.

## The seven years before the plan

**Change in trust-wide persistent absence, percentage points on the previous year:**

| 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| +1.8 | −0.6 | +2.3 | +0.4 | +1.1 | +2.9 | −0.2 |

**Safeguarding referrals to the local authority:**

| 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 11 | 6 | **13** | 9 | 7 | 10 |

## The four years since

| Year | Persistent-absence change | Safeguarding referrals | What the October report recorded |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2023 | +1.4 | 9 | Neither figure triggered |
| 2024 | **+4.7** | 11 | Absence rose more than four points; not two consecutive years; no trigger |
| 2025 | +2.2 | **14** | **Safeguarding referrals exceeded twelve** |
| 2026 | **+4.3** | 10 | Absence rose more than four points; not consecutive with 2024; no trigger |

**The October 2025 board minute records: "revision clause figures reported; no referral required."** The scoring model has not been re-specified.

## One further fact

**The model was trained on four years of attendance records to predict which pupils a head of year had referred to pastoral support.** Two of those four years were the pandemic years, when attendance recording changed twice and remote learning was recorded as present in some schools and absent in others.

## Produce

1. **Compute the baseline** for each of the two watched figures — mean, spread, and maximum — using the seven years before the plan.

2. **For each threshold, say whether it is a trigger or a timer**, and give the arithmetic. A threshold that sits inside the range of ordinary variation will fire on ordinary variation.

3. **Say what should have happened in October 2025**, and what you can and cannot conclude from the 2025 referral count.

4. **The absence limb was exceeded twice and never fired.** Say why, and say whether that is a fault in the rule or a cost that was accepted when it was written.

5. **Rewrite the "If" clause** so that it cannot be misread, and so that each threshold is defensible against the baseline.

6. **Look at the three monthly indicators.** Say what they can see and what they cannot, and name one failure that would leave all three looking acceptable.

7. **Compute the ratio of the two numbers collected by different teams**, for both years. Say what it shows and why nobody reported it.

8. **Diagnose the failure by the stage it entered through**, using the stages of this book. Say where the symptom appeared, where the failure entered, and how far apart they are.

9. **Write a revision trigger in two directions** — one condition about the world, and one about the model itself.
