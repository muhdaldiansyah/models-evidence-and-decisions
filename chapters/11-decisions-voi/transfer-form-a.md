# Chapter 11 Cold-Transfer Task — Form A

Status: reader-delivery copy. Governed by `spec.md` (Transfer target) and `transfer.md`.

Without consulting the Chapter 11 chapter text, the water case, or the rubric, work the situation below.

**You are advising the investment panel.** Produce a written recommendation.

Every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something you need is missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **50 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## The decision

A rail operator has a 12 km route section with rising rates of rail defects and two emergency speed restrictions imposed in the last year.

Engineering has identified two possible causes and cannot currently tell which is operating.

> **State 1 — rolling contact fatigue.** Surface cracking from wheel-rail contact. Grinding removes the damaged layer and arrests it.
>
> **State 2 — a formation defect.** The track bed beneath is failing, so the rail flexes and cracks. Grinding removes the symptom and the cracking returns.

An inspection register of comparable route sections records **5** cases resolved as rolling contact fatigue and **3** as formation defects.

## The acts

| | Act | Cost |
|---|---|---:|
| **A** | Full grinding programme on the affected section | **£850,000** |
| **B** | Rail replacement on the worst 4 km | **£3,200,000** |
| **C** | Permanent speed restriction on the worst sections, with monitoring | **£400,000** |

## The consequence table

Total cost over five years, in **£ thousands**, including the act's own cost and a monetised cost of remaining delay minutes, defect rectification, and reputational exposure. **Lower is better.**

| Act | If rolling contact fatigue | If a formation defect |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A** — grinding | **1,100** | **2,600** |
| **B** — rail replacement | **3,400** | **3,300** |
| **C** — restriction and monitoring | **2,000** | **2,000** |

## Two studies are on offer

**Study 1 — a combined ultrasonic and ground-penetrating radar survey. £45,000.**

It returns a positive result, indicating rolling contact fatigue, with probability **0.80** if that is what is operating, and with probability **0.25** if the cause is a formation defect.

**Study 2 — a full formation investigation with trial pits along the section. £280,000.**

The geotechnical team believe it would settle the question with near certainty.

## Produce

Write a response containing all eight items.

1. **Is any act dominated?** Say which and why, and what follows.
2. **Compute the expected cost of each act** at the register's odds.
3. **Name the rule you have just used**, and say what using it assumes.
4. **Does any act have no spread?** If so, say what a panel that wanted to avoid the worst case would choose and what it would cost them.
5. **Find the critical value** — at what probability does the best act change? Say how far the register's odds are from it.
6. **Compute the value of Study 1**, and say whether to commission it.
7. **Compute the ceiling** — what would it be worth to know the cause with certainty? Then say what that implies for **Study 2**, without computing anything further about it.
8. **State two things** the consequence table required somebody to decide, which are not measurements.

Finish with **your recommendation in four sentences**, naming what it is conditional on.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form B at all.**
