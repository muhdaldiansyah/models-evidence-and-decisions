# Chapter 11 Cold-Transfer Task — Form B

Status: reader-delivery copy. Governed by `spec.md` (Transfer target) and `transfer.md`.

Without consulting the Chapter 11 chapter text, the water case, or the rubric, work the situation below.

**You are advising the risk committee.** Produce a written recommendation.

Every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something you need is missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **50 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## The decision

A retailer's returns losses have risen sharply over two years. The loss-prevention team has identified two possible causes and cannot currently tell which is operating.

> **State 1 — organised fraud rings.** A small number of coordinated groups exploiting the returns process at scale, with detectable transaction patterns.
>
> **State 2 — diffuse individual abuse.** Large numbers of ordinary customers each abusing the policy occasionally, with no shared pattern to detect.

A register of past loss investigations across the group records **9** resolved as organised activity and **6** as diffuse abuse.

## The acts

| | Act | Cost |
|---|---|---:|
| **A** | Deploy the automated transaction-screening rule | **£180,000** |
| **B** | Stand up a manual review team for high-value returns | **£950,000** |
| **C** | Tighten the returns policy, with customer messaging | **£220,000** |

## The consequence table

Total cost over three years, in **£ thousands**, including the act's own cost and a monetised cost of remaining fraud losses, false-positive friction on legitimate customers, and lost goodwill. **Lower is better.**

| Act | If organised rings | If diffuse abuse |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A** — screening rule | **620** | **1,340** |
| **B** — manual review team | **1,700** | **1,650** |
| **C** — policy and messaging | **1,050** | **1,050** |

## Two studies are on offer

**Study 1 — a transaction-network analysis of the last eighteen months. £20,000.**

It returns a positive result, indicating organised activity, with probability **0.75** if that is what is operating, and with probability **0.20** if the losses are diffuse.

**Study 2 — a forensic audit with external investigators. £150,000.**

The audit firm believe it would settle the question with near certainty.

## Produce

Write a response containing all eight items.

1. **Is any act dominated?** Say which and why, and what follows.
2. **Compute the expected cost of each act** at the register's odds.
3. **Name the rule you have just used**, and say what using it assumes.
4. **Does any act have no spread?** If so, say what a committee that wanted to avoid the worst case would choose and what it would cost them.
5. **Find the critical value** — at what probability does the best act change? Say how far the register's odds are from it.
6. **Compute the value of Study 1**, and say whether to commission it.
7. **Compute the ceiling** — what would it be worth to know the cause with certainty? Then say what that implies for **Study 2**, without computing anything further about it.
8. **State two things** the consequence table required somebody to decide, which are not measurements.

Finish with **your recommendation in four sentences**, naming what it is conditional on.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form A at all.**
