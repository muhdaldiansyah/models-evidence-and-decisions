# Chapter 1 — Diagnosis Feedback

Open this file only **after** completing the planted-failure diagnosis in Section 6.

A strong response should identify consequential failures rather than merely collect labels. Plausible diagnoses include:

| Failure | Why it matters | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| “Water shortage” substituted for intended use | A topic does not specify what answer is needed or how it will be used | State the Utility Director's immediate decision and intended use |
| Dashboard record treated as physical target | The record can differ from the physical state that matters | Separate target, record, and observation process; verify when decision-sensitive |
| Prediction/association treated as intervention evidence | Historical pattern does not by itself establish what the conservation action will cause | State the intervention claim separately and acknowledge evidence limits |
| Alternatives reduced to pump more vs mandatory restriction | Feasible information-gathering, combined, staged, and voluntary actions disappear | Reopen the alternative set |
| Decision authority ignored | The director cannot directly impose the mandatory restriction | Reframe as a request/escalation to the City Manager |
| Values and stakeholders omitted | “Safer” is treated as self-justifying without showing burdens, costs, or who bears them | State material consequences and evaluative premises |
| Recommendation treated as the decision | Advice is confused with authorization/commitment by the decision-maker | Separate analysis, recommendation, and decision |
| Responsive environment treated as passive | A conservation action can change future demand and invalidate a no-new-action forecast | Monitor and revise the forecast/process representation after deployment |
| Reasoning treated as a one-way sequence | Later evidence cannot repair earlier framing under a waterfall interpretation | Allow backward revision when evidence or response exposes failure |

There is no single required ordering of repairs. A defensible priority explains why one repair changes several downstream conclusions. In this case, correcting the intended use/decision and the current-state evidence are especially consequential because they govern what later analysis is for and whether the apparent reserve margin is real.

Do not convert this feedback into a checklist that must be applied mechanically to every problem.
