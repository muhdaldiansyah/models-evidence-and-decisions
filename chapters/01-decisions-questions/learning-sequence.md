# Chapter 1 Learning-Sequence Control

Status: governed by `../../decisions/0008-chapter1-pedagogical-scaffold.md`

This is an authoring-control artifact. It does not create a new reader-facing framework or acronym.

## Core sequence

Use this progression:

`attempt → worked example → self-explain → fade → diagnose → independently produce → retrieve → delayed retest`

The sequence is a book-specific pedagogical synthesis.

## Phase 1 — Initial unsupported attempt

Give the learner a short underspecified consequential problem before the full worked analysis.

Requirements:

- no Chapter 1 checklist in view;
- no scoring rubric in view;
- preserve the learner's response;
- do not score it as an achievement measure;
- proceed soon into the worked example so major errors receive corrective information;
- return to the initial response later for comparison.

Current design parameter:

- approximately five minutes.

Pilot:

- completion rate;
- whether the response is long enough to expose default framing habits;
- whether five minutes causes random guessing rather than meaningful commitment.

## Phase 2 — Complete worked water-supply case

Show the full first-pass performance.

The worked case should expose:

- intended use and decision situation;
- target and material qualifiers;
- claim type;
- target system, model, measurement, and records distinctions;
- evidence limitations;
- alternatives, consequences, and evaluative premises;
- dynamics, feedback, response, and monitoring;
- rival formulation;
- at least two materially different backward revisions;
- routing to later chapters or specialist expertise.

Do not present only a polished final answer.

Show why each major reasoning move occurs and what would have gone wrong under a plausible rival framing.

The two governed backward revisions remain:

1. telemetry/sensor problem makes dashboard storage diverge from verified physical storage;
2. conservation action changes demand enough that the pre-action forecast is no longer adequate.

The count of two is an instructional design choice for the worked example, not a universal rule.

## Phase 3 — Principle-focused self-explanation

Use a small number of high-value pauses.

Good prompts ask the learner to explain:

- why a dashboard reading is not automatically the target;
- why prediction does not establish an intervention effect;
- why evidence about consequences does not by itself determine a decision;
- why a later observation forces revision rather than a mere numerical update;
- why one alternative framing is more appropriate for the stated use.

Avoid prompts whose best response is mere paraphrase.

Current design parameter:

- three pauses.

Pilot:

- code responses for principle connection versus paraphrase;
- measure completion time and fatigue;
- test whether the third pause adds diagnostic value.

## Phase 4 — Structural contrasts

Use contrasts only when they expose a reasoning relation.

Approved contrast families include:

- same system, different intended use;
- same outcome language, different claim type;
- physical state versus observed record;
- dynamic non-strategic process versus responsive/possibly strategic environment;
- evidence about consequences versus evaluation and choice.

The pendulum remains the primary clean physical contrast.
The student-assessment case remains a compact measurement/institutional contrast.

Do not add examples merely to increase domain count.

## Phase 5 — Faded reasoning support

Provide a compact case or case variation with reduced reasoning prompts.

Fade items such as:

- explicit target prompt;
- claim-type reminder;
- positive/normative reminder;
- reminder to search for alternatives;
- reminder to screen time/feedback/response;
- routing hints.

Do not fade information needed to solve the case, including:

- decision authority;
- operational limits;
- case-specific measurement facts;
- supplied eligibility or policy rules;
- material constraints;
- required timing facts.

The goal is to reduce scaffolding while keeping the domain task fully specified.

Pair fading with self-explanation of the underlying reasoning principle.

## Phase 6 — Planted-failure diagnosis and repair

Present a concise analysis containing multiple consequential framing failures.

Approved failure classes include:

- topic substituted for intended use;
- record, label, or metric treated as the target;
- a material target qualifier omitted;
- association or predictive performance treated as sufficient for an intervention effect;
- recommendation presented without material consequences, values, or alternatives;
- recommendation treated as identical to the decision;
- deployment assumed not to change behavior despite an explicit responsive environment;
- Reasoning Loop treated as a mandatory waterfall.

For each selected failure, require:

1. diagnosis;
2. consequence;
3. repair.

When useful, also ask which failure should be fixed first and why.

Do not score only the number of errors named.

## Phase 7 — Independent cold transfer

Use one of the parallel forms in `transfer.md`.

Requirements:

- no Chapter 1 checklist visible;
- no worked solution visible;
- no scoring rubric visible until production is complete;
- same core outputs across both forms;
- same scoring dimensions;
- all necessary domain facts supplied;
- no requirement for external refrigeration, housing-law, or emergency-management expertise.

Form A:

- refrigerated warehouse cooling risk;
- mainly physical/engineering;
- emphasizes state, sensing, delay, feedback, and intervention;
- strategic interpretation not required unless prompt facts support it.

Form B:

- emergency temporary-housing allocation after a flood;
- institutional/policy;
- emphasizes target population, records/selection, alternatives, values, delay, and adaptive or strategic response when warranted.

Current design parameter:

- 30–40 minutes for a one-page first-pass analysis.

Pilot actual completion time rather than treating this interval as a validated standard.

## Phase 8 — Post-task self-explanation

After rubric or answer comparison, require a short response:

> What was the most consequential revision you made after checking your answer, and why?

The learner should identify a reasoning change, not merely say that more detail was added.

## Phase 9 — Retrieval from memory

Hide the navigation map.

Ask the learner to reconstruct:

- the five-part navigation structure;
- important backward links;
- intended use versus target;
- evidence versus claim type;
- consequence claims versus evaluative premises;
- action versus feedback/response;
- monitoring versus revision/routing.

Then compare against the canonical map and repair omissions.

Do not replace this task with rereading or recognition questions.

## Phase 10 — Delayed retest

Use the other parallel transfer form after a delay.

Current operational window:

- 7–14 days.

This is a pilot schedule, not a universal spacing optimum.

For self-study:

- recommend doing the less familiar domain first;
- record a retest date;
- reuse the same rubric after production.

For pilots:

- counterbalance form order;
- compare dimension-level scores;
- record order effects and timing;
- do not infer form equivalence from surface similarity alone.

## Active-learning guardrail

Keep the existing authoring target that at least half of active-learning time involves prediction, production, self-explanation, error diagnosis, or retrieval rather than rereading.

Treat `50%` as an authoring guardrail pending pilot data, not as an evidence-backed universal threshold.

Track actual active-learning minutes in timed pilots.

## Rubric and feedback

The eight existing rubric dimensions remain diagnostic.

Prioritize dimension-level feedback over total score.

No validated aggregate cut score is assumed.

The opening attempt is not scored as achievement; it is retained for qualitative before/after comparison.

Cold-transfer production is completed before rubric consultation.

## Transfer claim boundary

Chapter 1 aims to establish an initial capability for independent transfer and to diagnose where transfer fails.

Do not claim that four hours of instruction establishes durable far transfer.

Later repeated first-pass tasks after Chapter 5 and in Chapter 16 provide stronger evidence of development.

## Source boundary

Load-bearing Research 07 sources:

- `sweller1985worked` — complete worked performance before full independent production;
- `chi1994selfexplanation` — prompted self-explanation;
- `atkinson2003fading` — fading combined with principle-focused self-explanation;
- `schwartz2011contrasting` — structural contrasting cases;
- `roediger2006testing` — delayed retrieval rather than rereading alone;
- `butler2010transfer` — retrieval for inferential and transfer-oriented performance.

Do not add every contextual learning-science source to the bibliography unless a manuscript claim later needs it.

## Pilot parameters

The following remain explicitly open to timed/reader pilot revision:

- five-minute opening attempt;
- three self-explanation pauses;
- 50% active-learning guardrail;
- 30–40 minute cold-transfer target;
- 7–14 day delayed-retest window.

Pilot evidence may change these values without reopening the underlying scaffold decision.
