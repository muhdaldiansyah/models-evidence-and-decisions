# Chapter 1 Pilot Protocol

Status: formative pilot protocol; operationalizes governed Chapter 1 design parameters and does not create validated assessment claims.

Governed by:

- `spec.md`;
- `learning-sequence.md`;
- `transfer.md`;
- `transfer-form-a.md`;
- `transfer-form-b.md`;
- `transfer-rubric.md`;
- Decision 0008.

This protocol is for Chapter 1 revision and freeze decisions. It is not a psychometric validation study and does not assume that the rubric has a validated aggregate score or cut point.

## 1. Pilot questions

The pilot should answer practical authoring questions.

1. Can a serious reader complete the full Chapter 1 experience within the governed **4-hour** target without weakening the independent production task?
2. Does the approximately **5-minute** opening attempt produce a meaningful unsupported framing rather than random guessing or an empty response?
3. Do the **three self-explanation pauses** elicit principle-focused reasoning rather than paraphrase, and does each add enough value to justify its time and fatigue cost?
4. Does at least half of active-learning time involve prediction, production, self-explanation, diagnosis, or retrieval rather than rereading, consistent with the current **50% authoring guardrail**?
5. Can readers produce a one-page first-pass analysis on an unfamiliar transfer form within the current **30–40 minute** pilot target without consulting the checklist, worked solution, or rubric?
6. Do Form A and Form B expose the same core reasoning dimensions without one form being dominated by hidden domain knowledge or surface complexity?
7. After **7–14 days**, can the reader produce the same reasoning structure on the previously unseen parallel form without rereading Chapter 1 first?
8. Which rubric dimensions, major category errors, or navigation relationships remain weak enough to require manuscript or exercise revision before freeze?

## 2. What the pilot may and may not establish

### The pilot may support authoring decisions about

- chapter completion time;
- local pacing and fatigue;
- clarity of instructions;
- whether a prompt supplies enough domain information;
- whether a scaffold should be retained, reduced, moved, or removed;
- whether a distinction is consistently misunderstood;
- whether one transfer form appears materially harder for reasons unrelated to the intended reasoning task;
- whether the current timing parameters should be revised;
- whether rubric dimensions provide useful diagnostic feedback;
- whether the delayed task reveals retention/transfer weaknesses worth addressing.

### The pilot does not by itself establish

- a validated aggregate Chapter 1 score;
- a pass/fail cut score;
- psychometric equivalence of Forms A and B;
- durable far transfer after four hours;
- a universal optimal spacing interval;
- a universal optimal number of self-explanation prompts;
- causal effectiveness of the chapter relative to another instructional design.

## 3. Participant preparation

Use readers who can meet the chapter's stated prerequisites but do not require prior probability, causal inference, decision theory, control, game theory, or domain expertise.

Before the session, record only information needed to interpret the pilot:

- participant ID or pseudonym;
- broad quantitative/technical training level;
- prior familiarity with water utilities;
- prior familiarity with refrigerated warehousing/cooling;
- prior familiarity with emergency housing/disaster allocation;
- whether English technical prose at the chapter's level is comfortable;
- any interruption or accessibility condition likely to affect timing.

Do not treat domain familiarity as a nuisance to hide. It is a diagnostic variable for whether the transfer forms demand hidden knowledge.

## 4. Form-order assignment

Counterbalance the transfer forms across pilot readers.

- Order **A→B**: first cold transfer uses Form A; delayed retest uses Form B.
- Order **B→A**: first cold transfer uses Form B; delayed retest uses Form A.

For self-study rather than organized pilots, retain the chapter rule: use the less familiar domain first and reserve the other form for the delayed retest.

Do not let a reader preview the delayed form.

## 5. Session 1 — timed chapter + first cold transfer

### 5.1 Start conditions

- Reader begins with the current `chapter.md` and normal access to the chapter's reader-facing links.
- Do not provide the Chapter 1 rubric, answer feedback, or transfer form in advance.
- Ask the reader not to search external domain information while completing the chapter or transfer task.
- Record actual start/end timestamps and meaningful interruptions rather than relying only on retrospective time estimates.

### 5.2 Opening unsupported attempt

Current parameter: approximately **5 minutes**.

Capture:

- start and stop time;
- whether all four opening prompts receive a meaningful response;
- whether the response is substantive enough to expose a default framing habit;
- whether the reader reports guessing because the time is too short;
- the preserved response itself.

Do **not** score the opening response as achievement.

### 5.3 Reading and worked-example timing

Record timestamps at least at the end of each numbered section.

For Sections 1–5, capture:

- section completion time;
- any rereading or backtracking that materially affects time;
- any sentence/table/exercise that the reader marks as confusing;
- any point where the reader believes outside domain knowledge is required;
- any point where the reader treats the five-part navigation as a mandatory sequence rather than a revisable map.

Do not interrupt the reader simply to collect more data unless the protocol requires an immediate response.

### 5.4 Three self-explanation pauses

For each governed pause, preserve the response before the manuscript explanation is read.

Code the response after the session as:

- **principle connection** — explains the underlying reasoning relation;
- **partial principle** — points in the right direction but leaves the relation incomplete;
- **paraphrase/label only** — mostly repeats nearby wording or names a term;
- **off-target / no response**.

Also record time spent and a short fatigue/annoyance note if volunteered.

Do not convert these codes into a validated score.

### 5.5 Planted-failure diagnosis

The learner completes the diagnosis before opening `diagnosis-feedback.md`.

Capture:

- diagnosis/consequence/repair response;
- which failure the learner selects as the first repair and why;
- whether any major category error goes unnoticed;
- whether the learner names errors without explaining consequences or repairs;
- time spent.

Do not interpret the raw number of named errors as the main outcome.

### 5.6 First cold-transfer production

Use the assigned first form only.

Rules:

- no Chapter 1 checklist;
- no worked water solution;
- no `transfer-rubric.md` in view;
- no preview of the delayed form;
- no external domain lookup;
- preserve the original one-page response before any revision.

Current production target: **30–40 minutes**.

Record:

- form used;
- start/end time;
- whether the reader requests missing domain facts;
- whether any supplied fact is misunderstood;
- whether external specialist knowledge appears necessary despite the prompt;
- original response.

### 5.7 Post-production rubric

Only after production, open `transfer-rubric.md`.

Record all eight **dimension-level scores (0–2)** separately:

1. intended use and decision;
2. target and context;
3. question and claim type;
4. model, measurement, and records;
5. evidence limits;
6. values and alternatives;
7. dynamics and response;
8. revision and routing.

Also mark each major category error as present/absent/not-applicable.

An aggregate total may be stored for exploratory convenience, but do not use it as a cut score or primary interpretation.

Preserve the required post-task self-explanation:

> What was the most consequential revision you would make after checking your answer, and why?

Code whether it identifies a reasoning change rather than merely “more detail.”

### 5.8 Retrieval from memory

Before the reader looks back at the chapter map, capture the reconstructed five-part navigation and backward links.

Record whether the reader can reconstruct, in their own words:

- five-part navigation;
- intended use versus target;
- evidence versus claim type;
- consequence claims versus evaluative premises;
- action versus feedback/response;
- monitoring versus revision/routing.

Record omissions and repairs after comparison.

### 5.9 End-of-session reader debrief

Use short, non-leading questions:

- Where did you feel you were solving the wrong task or unsure what was being asked?
- Which distinction changed how you approached the transfer problem most?
- Which section felt repetitive or disproportionately long?
- Which instruction was ambiguous?
- Where, if anywhere, did you need domain knowledge that the chapter did not supply?
- Did any table, link, or answer reveal appear before you were ready to see it?
- What did you fail to represent in your opening attempt that you later considered decision-relevant?

Record comments verbatim when practical, but treat them as reader observations rather than automatically correct diagnoses of the instructional cause.

## 6. Active-learning time audit

Classify timed activity into two broad buckets.

### Active production/retrieval

Include time spent on:

- opening prediction/production;
- self-explanation;
- faded-case production;
- planted-failure diagnosis and repair;
- cold-transfer production;
- post-task self-explanation;
- retrieval from memory.

### Reading/review

Include time spent primarily reading exposition, worked explanation, tables, or feedback without producing/retrieving an answer.

Compute:

`active-learning share = active production/retrieval time ÷ total active-learning time`

Treat **50%** as the current authoring guardrail, not a universal evidence-backed threshold. If timing classification is ambiguous, record the ambiguity rather than manufacturing precision.

## 7. Session 2 — delayed parallel-form retest

Schedule the second session **7–14 days** after Session 1.

Before the retest:

- do not ask the reader to reread Chapter 1;
- do not display the map, checklist, first transfer response, rubric, or feedback;
- confirm that the delayed form was not previewed;
- record the actual elapsed days.

Use the previously unseen parallel form and the same one-page production instruction.

Capture the same measures as the first cold transfer:

- form;
- start/end time;
- domain-knowledge issues;
- original response;
- eight dimension-level rubric scores after production;
- major category errors;
- post-task self-explanation.

Also record whether the reader spontaneously reconstructs relevant relationships without prompting.

## 8. Comparison rules

Interpret results at the **dimension and failure-pattern level first**.

For each participant compare:

- first versus delayed completion time;
- each of the eight rubric dimensions;
- major category errors;
- post-task self-explanation quality;
- domain-familiarity notes;
- form order.

Across participants, inspect:

- whether the same dimensions repeatedly fail;
- whether one form produces substantially different failure patterns;
- whether the difference tracks form order or domain familiarity;
- whether timing problems cluster in a particular chapter section or exercise;
- whether the third self-explanation pause adds distinct diagnostic value;
- whether readers treat the navigation as a waterfall;
- whether hidden domain knowledge rather than reasoning appears to drive transfer performance.

Do not declare Forms A and B equivalent because their average totals look similar. Do not infer a learning effect from Session 1→2 improvement without considering form/order differences.

## 9. Revision triggers

The pilot should trigger manuscript or exercise review when evidence suggests any of the following:

- a prompt systematically elicits a different task than intended;
- readers repeatedly conflate a record/metric with the target despite the worked example;
- prediction is repeatedly used as intervention evidence;
- recommendations repeatedly hide evaluative premises or authority;
- the navigation is repeatedly treated as a mandatory waterfall;
- a transfer form requires unsupplied specialist knowledge;
- one form's difficulty is dominated by its domain rather than the shared reasoning task;
- answer feedback or rubric becomes visible before production;
- the full chapter routinely exceeds the 4-hour budget because of exposition rather than productive activity;
- the 5-minute opening attempt produces mostly random guessing;
- one or more self-explanation pauses add fatigue without diagnostic or learning value;
- cold-transfer timing routinely makes a defensible one-page response impractical;
- delayed performance exposes a recurring structural omission that the chapter never repairs.

A trigger requires author adjudication; it does not automatically prescribe the repair.

## 10. Author adjudication after each pilot wave

After a coherent pilot wave, produce a short adjudication note separating:

1. **observed evidence** — timing, responses, dimension patterns, reader comments;
2. **interpretation** — likely instructional or domain cause;
3. **decision** — retain, revise, investigate, or defer;
4. **scope** — manuscript wording, exercise delivery, transfer form, rubric, timing parameter, or no change;
5. **follow-up evidence needed**.

Do not silently tune the chapter between participants in the same comparison wave unless a defect makes the task unusable. Record emergency fixes explicitly.

## 11. Freeze-facing outputs

Before Chapter 1 is declared verified/frozen, the pilot record should make it possible to answer:

- Was the 4-hour chapter target feasible for serious readers?
- Did the opening attempt expose useful baseline framing habits?
- Did the three self-explanation pauses earn their place?
- Was the 50% active-learning guardrail approximately achieved?
- Did readers complete independent cold transfer without visible scaffolds?
- Were both transfer forms usable without hidden domain expertise?
- What dimension-level weaknesses remained?
- What happened after the 7–14 day delayed retest?
- Which revisions were made in response to pilot evidence?
- Is there any evidence justifying an aggregate score or cut point? Default answer remains **no** unless evidence specifically supports one.

Human water-domain SME review remains a separate publication gate. Pilot success does not substitute for domain realism review, and SME approval does not substitute for learner-pilot evidence.
