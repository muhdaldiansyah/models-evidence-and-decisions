## Form A — the port authority

### The ranking

**P 2.500, Q 0.500, U1 0.431, U2 0.411, R 0.341, S 0.284, T 0.229.**

Delay-hours per £1,000. Any consistent unit is fine; the order is what matters.

**Check that U1 sits immediately above U2.** If your ranking has them far apart, recompute — the closeness is the trap, and 0.431 against 0.411 is a difference most people would round away.

### Funding down the list

**P (60), Q (580), U1 (1,880), then U2 is unavailable, then R (2,760). S would take it to 3,780 and T to 3,940.**

**Portfolio P+Q+U1+R. Spend £2,760k. Delivers 1,270. Unspent £840k.**

If you stopped at P+Q+U1 without testing R, reread the instruction — the rule is to keep going down the list, not to stop at the first item that does not fit.

**The £840k is the visible symptom.** The cheapest remaining scheme is S at £1,020k, so nothing on the list can absorb it. Money that cannot buy anything is the signature of a ranking that stopped in the wrong place.

### Where the ranking fails

**The best affordable portfolio is P+Q+U2 = 1,560, spending £3,380k.**

**The ranking misses it by 290** — about 19% of what was available.

The explanation the rubric is looking for:

**Taking U1 rather than U2 leaves £1,720k, and the best the remaining list can do with £1,720k is R at 300.** The £1,500k more that U2 costs buys 590. So the higher ratio bought the worse programme, and the ratio could not have told you, because a ratio evaluates a scheme against its own cost and never against what the rest of the money could otherwise become.

**Credit an answer that says the ratio is correct and is answering a different question.** That is stronger than saying the ratio is approximate.

**Do not credit** an answer that finds the optimum by search and does not explain the failure. Item 3 asks for both.

### Pricing the constraint

| Change | Best portfolio | Delivers | Difference |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| £3,300k (−300) | P+U2 | 1,300 | **−260** |
| £3,400k (−200) | P+Q+U2 | 1,560 | **0** |
| £3,600k | P+Q+U2 | 1,560 | — |
| £3,700k (+100) | P+Q+U2 | 1,560 | **0** |
| £3,800k (+200) | P+R+U2 | 1,600 | **+40** |
| £4,260k (+660) | P+Q+R+U2 | 1,860 | **+300** |

**Four things a strong answer draws out.**

**An extra £100k buys nothing** — and that is a fact about £100k, not about the envelope. The optimum already leaves £220k unspent, so the first £100k joins money that is already idle.

**An extra £200k buys 40**, at 0.20 delay-hours per £1,000, by swapping Q for R.

**The first £200k of cuts is free**, because the slack absorbs it.

**The next £100k of cuts costs 260**, because at £3,300k the portfolio cannot hold both Q and U2 and has to drop to P+U2.

**The pattern is the answer.** The price of the constraint is not a number; it is a staircase, and where you happen to probe determines what you conclude. An answer that reports a single figure has missed the item even if the figure is right.

**And the asymmetry matters practically.** Cutting £300k costs 260. Adding £200k gains 40. Anyone negotiating this envelope should know that losing is more than four times as expensive per pound — 0.87 delay-hours per £1,000 cut against 0.20 per £1,000 added — as winning is profitable.

### The regret table

Optima by future: **as forecast P+Q+U2 = 1,560; larger vessels P+Q+U2 = 2,340; feeder shift Q+R+S+T = 1,680.**

| Portfolio | Spend | As forecast | Larger vessels | Feeder shift | Largest |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| P+Q+U2 | 3,380 | 0 | 0 | 970 | **970** |
| Q+R+S+T | 3,600 | 440 | 1,380 | 0 | **1,380** |
| **P+R+S+U1** | 3,260 | 260 | 560 | 490 | **560** |
| P+Q+R+U1 (the ranking's) | 2,760 | 290 | 590 | 550 | **590** |

Arithmetic check: P+R+S+U1 delivers 1,300 / 1,780 / 1,190 across the three futures.

**Two futures share an optimum.** If your table has three distinct optimal portfolios, recheck — the deepening is best both as forecast and under larger vessels, and only the feeder shift overturns it.

### The robust portfolio

**P+R+S+U1, largest regret 560.**

**It is optimal in none of the three futures.** An answer that treats that as a reason to reject it has not understood the criterion: being second-best everywhere is what a hedge looks like, and no portfolio that is best somewhere is anything but badly wrong somewhere else here.

**If the central forecast is right, choosing it costs 260 delay-hours a year** — 1,560 against 1,300, about a sixth of what was available.

**Strong answers say that number out loud.** Robustness is a purchase, and the item asks for the price.

**Also credit** an answer noticing that the ranking's own portfolio, P+Q+R+U1, has a largest regret of 590 — very close to the minimax. The ranking is a poor optimiser and an accidentally decent hedge, because it happens to pick the phase over the full build. That is luck, not method, and an answer that says so has seen something real.

### The adaptive plan

**What is committed now:** P, R, S, and phase 1 of the deepening.

**What is deferred:** the second deepening phase, £1,650k.

**A signpost needs four parts.** *The share of calls made by vessels with a design draught over 12.5 m, measured from the authority's own arrivals record, reviewed quarterly, triggering commitment when it exceeds 25% across any rolling twelve months.*

**Check yours has all four.** An observable quantity somebody already records; a threshold with a number; a named owner; a frequency. A signpost missing the threshold is a review date, and a review date is not a signpost — it tells you when to think, not what to think.

**The premium.** £150k buys the ability to not commit £1,500k to a deepening that the feeder shift would make nearly worthless — where U2 delivers 130 and U1 delivers 90, so almost the whole of the second phase's value disappears. That is the trade the item is asking you to state: 10% of the deferred cost, against the risk of spending all of it on the wrong thing.

**A strong answer prices it as a fraction and says what it protects against.** An answer that names the premium without saying what it buys has done half the item.

### Value judgments

Any two of:

- **What an hour of vessel delay is worth**, and to whom — the line, the shipper, the port, the local economy. The column is denominated in a unit that hides a price.
- **Whose delay counts.** A delay-hour on a 14,000-TEU ship and one on a coastal feeder are counted the same.
- **The horizon.** Benefits are annual and schemes have different lives; a shore-power installation and a dredged channel do not depreciate alike.
- **That delay is the objective at all.** Emissions, safety, and landside congestion appear nowhere.

**Do not credit** "the forecast is uncertain" — that is an estimate with a provenance, not a value judgment. The distinction is Chapter 10's and it is being tested here.

### The recommendation

Four sentences, and it must actually recommend. A strong one names the portfolio, names the deferred commitment and its signpost, states what it costs if the forecast is right, and names what it is conditional on.

**Declining to recommend because there are no probabilities is the failure this item exists to catch.**

## The trap in both forms

**The ranking's portfolio looks defensible.** Four schemes, funded in order of value for money, each individually justified, money left over that reads as prudence rather than as failure.

**Nothing about it announces a problem**, and the better portfolio is never seen because nobody solves for it.

If you funded down the list and moved on to item 4 without testing item 3 properly, you did what the chapter says organisations do. That is the finding, not a criticism — and the defence is a habit rather than a suspicion, because there is no symptom to be suspicious about.

## Four answers that look right and are not

**"The budget is not binding — an extra £100,000 buys nothing."**
One probe of a staircase. The £220k or £250k of slack means the first increment always buys nothing, and it says nothing about £600k.

**"The robust portfolio is worse in every future, so it should be rejected."**
It is worse in every future by construction. That is what a hedge is. The comparison that matters is against the largest regret of the alternatives, and there it wins.

**"We should assign probabilities to the three futures and take the expectation."**
You may, and the form does not forbid it — but nobody in either organisation would supply them, and inventing them converts a stated ignorance into a number that will be quoted without its provenance. Chapter 11's machinery is not wrong here; its inputs are absent.

**"The plan is adaptive — the second phase will be reviewed in year three."**
A date, not a signpost. It says when somebody will think, not what would change their mind.

## A note on tone

Both forms hand you a programme that a competent team has already worked up, and the items ask you to find that its ranking is wrong.

**The team is not incompetent.** Ranking by value for money is what nearly every capital-programme guide recommends, it is correct when schemes are small relative to the envelope, and the failure it produces here is silent. The chapter's claim is about a condition, not about the people.
