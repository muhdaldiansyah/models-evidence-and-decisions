# Chapter 12 Cold-Transfer Task — Form A

Allow about **50 minutes**.

Work from this page alone.
Do not open the chapter, the water case, the rubric, or Form B.
Every fact you need is here. Do not look anything up.

## The situation

A **port authority** operates a single commercial harbour with four berths, a rail-connected quay, and an approach channel.
Vessels regularly wait outside the harbour — for a berth, for a tide deep enough to enter, or for a tug.
The authority measures this as **vessel delay-hours**, and last year recorded about 4,100 of them.

The authority's five-year capital plan allocates **£3,600,000** to harbour improvement.
That figure came out of a corporate allocation across the authority's whole estate, made eighteen months ago, before any of the schemes below were specified.

Seven schemes have been worked up.
The engineering team has estimated, for each, the **annual vessel delay-hours it would avoid** under the authority's central traffic forecast.

## The schemes

| | Scheme | Cost (£k) | Delay-hours avoided per year |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **P** | Fender and mooring renewal, berths 1–2 | 60 | 150 |
| **Q** | Tug contract restructure and third tug | 520 | 260 |
| **R** | Berth 3 strengthening and re-decking | 880 | 300 |
| **S** | Rail siding link to the quay | 1,020 | 290 |
| **T** | Shore power installation | 1,180 | 270 |
| **U1** | Channel deepening — **phase 1 only** (to 12.5 m) | 1,300 | 560 |
| **U2** | Channel deepening — **full scheme** (to 14.0 m) | 2,800 | 1,150 |

**U1 and U2 are mutually exclusive.** U2 is the whole deepening; U1 is its first phase.
Any scheme is either built or not — there are no part-schemes and no shared costs.

**If phase 1 is built now and the deepening is completed later, the second phase costs £1,650,000** — against £1,500,000 if the full scheme is built in one go. Remobilising a dredging contractor and re-consenting the deeper works accounts for the difference.

## Three futures

The traffic forecast is the authority's own, and the commercial director has said in writing that she would not defend it beyond three years. Two other futures are considered credible by the harbourmaster. Nobody will put a probability on any of them.

The same seven schemes, under each future:

| Scheme | As forecast | Larger vessels | Feeder shift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P | 150 | 120 | 200 |
| Q | 260 | 220 | 380 |
| R | 300 | 260 | 460 |
| S | 290 | 250 | 440 |
| T | 270 | 230 | 400 |
| U1 | 560 | 1,150 | 90 |
| U2 | 1,150 | 2,000 | 130 |

**As forecast** — traffic and vessel sizes continue on the trend of the last six years.

**Larger vessels** — the two main container lines cascade larger ships onto this route, as they have on two comparable routes. Draught becomes the binding constraint on almost every call.

**Feeder shift** — the lines consolidate deep-sea calls at a larger hub and serve this port with smaller, more frequent feeder vessels. Draught stops mattering; berth turnaround and landside connection become the constraint.

## Produce

1. **Rank the seven schemes** by delay-hours avoided per pound, using the *as forecast* column.

2. **Fund down that ranking** until no further scheme fits within £3,600,000. Report the portfolio, what it spends, what it delivers, and what is left unspent.

3. **Find the best portfolio the budget can buy** under the *as forecast* column. If it differs from your answer to item 2, say by how much and explain **why the ranking produced a different portfolio**.

4. **Price the budget constraint**, using the *as forecast* column. What would an extra £100,000 buy? An extra £200,000? What would a £200,000 cut cost, and a £300,000 cut? Say what the pattern of those four answers tells you.

5. **Build a regret table** over the three futures for these four portfolios: P+Q+U2; Q+R+S+T; P+R+S+U1; and your answer to item 2. Report each portfolio's regret in each future and its largest regret.

6. **Identify the portfolio with the smallest largest regret.** Say how many of the three futures it is the best portfolio in. State what choosing it costs the authority if the central forecast turns out to be right.

7. **Turn one portfolio into an adaptive plan.** Name what is committed now, what is deferred, and **one signpost** — the observable quantity, the threshold, who measures it, and how often — that would trigger the deferred commitment. Say what the £150,000 staging premium buys.

8. **Name two value judgments** buried in the delay-hours column, neither of which is an engineering estimate.

**Then write a recommendation of no more than four sentences**, naming what it is conditional on.
