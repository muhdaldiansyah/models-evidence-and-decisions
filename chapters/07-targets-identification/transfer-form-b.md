# Chapter 7 Cold-Transfer Task — Form B

Status: reader-delivery copy. Governed by `spec.md` (Transfer target) and `transfer.md`.

Without consulting the Chapter 7 chapter text, the water case, or the rubric, work the situation below.

**You are the analyst.** Produce a written examination of the claim.

Every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something you need is missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **50 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## The claim

A city monitors **eighteen bus corridors**. Over a four-year programme, seven of them received a bus lane.

The transport authority's evaluation contains this sentence:

> **The bus lane programme reduced journey times.**

The committee is deciding whether to fund bus lanes on the remaining eleven corridors.

## The record

Mean peak-period journey time, end to end, in minutes, before the programme and after:

| | Corridors | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bus lane | **7** | **34.5** | **30.2** |
| No bus lane | **11** | **21.8** | **21.0** |

## Four supplied facts

**1. How the seven were chosen.** The corridors selected were the **seven slowest at the time the programme was funded**. This was the stated allocation policy and appeared in the programme business case.

**2. What else changed.** A **city-wide signal-retiming programme** ran across all eighteen corridors during the same four years. It was not part of the bus lane programme.

**3. What "bus lane" covers.** The programme's records list all seven corridors as having *a bus lane*. Four different arrangements were built:

| Option | What was built |
|---|---|
| 1 | A 24-hour bus-only lane |
| 2 | A peak-hours-only bus lane |
| 3 | A bus-and-taxi lane, all hours |
| 4 | A bus-and-cycle lane, all hours |

The records do not say which corridor received which. Traffic officers note that on the busiest corridor the bus-and-taxi lane carries enough taxis at peak that buses queue in it.

**4. A constraint on where a lane can go.** A bus lane requires a carriageway at least **9.5 metres** wide. All seven corridors that received one are **11 metres or wider**.

Four of the eleven remaining corridors are **7 to 8 metre historic streets**, which cannot take a lane at all. Those four are the slowest of the eleven, and they are the corridors the committee is most concerned about.

## Produce

Write a response containing all seven items.

1. **State the target quantity** the evaluation's sentence is making a claim about. Give the treatment, the comparison, the population, the variable, the time window, and the summary. Where the sentence does not say, choose and mark your choice.
2. **Compute every comparison the four numbers support.** There is more than one. State each result and say in one sentence what it appears to show.
3. **Say why the cross-sectional comparison points the way it does.** Use fact 1.
4. **Say what fact 2 does** to the before-and-after comparison, and to the comparison of the changes.
5. **Fact 3.** Say what problem it creates for the claim, and be specific about which of the four options might behave differently from the others and why.
6. **Fact 4.** State what it means for the committee's actual question. Then answer this directly: **would the same programme run over twenty years, on a hundred corridors, fix this problem?** Say why or why not.
7. **Write the experiment that would answer the committee's question.** Give its eligibility, intervention, outcome, follow-up, and comparison. Then say why it cannot be run, and — the important part — **what writing it tells you about the assumption the existing analysis is carrying.**

Finish with a **verdict** in three short paragraphs: what is not established, which assumption would change that, and what you would go and get.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form A at all.**
