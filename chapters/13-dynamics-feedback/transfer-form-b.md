## The situation

A **municipal district heating network** burns wood pellets to heat about 4,000 homes and a hospital.

Pellets arrive by road on a standing daily delivery.
Pellets leave when they are burned.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Silo capacity (deliveries above this are turned away at the gate) | **780 t** |
| Pre-winter fill level | **700 t** |
| Critical level — below this the backup gas boiler must be run, at four times the fuel cost | **250 t** |
| Standing daily delivery | **180 t/day** |
| Ordinary daily burn | **180 t/day** |

**The silo held 700 t at the start of day 1.**

## Two things the operator cannot do instantly

**The silo figure is two days old.** Silo level is measured continuously by load cell, but the figure is reconciled against weighbridge tickets before it is used for ordering, because the load cells drift. Reconciliation takes two days. The most recent figure any ordering decision can use describes the silo as it was two days earlier.

**A change to the delivery takes two days to arrive.** The haulier schedules vehicles a day ahead and the supplier loads from a yard 90 minutes away. A delivery changed today changes what arrives in two days' time.

## The week

A cold snap raised the burn for seven days.

| Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 onward |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Tonnes burned | 240 | 270 | 300 | 288 | 258 | 216 | 198 | **180** |

The delivery was left at the standing 180 t/day throughout the week, because the duty operator had no authority to vary the contract and the network manager was at a conference.

## The network's written rule

The operating manual has contained this paragraph for six years.

> When the most recent verified silo figure is below **350 t**, set the daily delivery to the most recent verified burn plus **60 t**. When the most recent verified silo figure is above **580 t**, return the delivery to the standing 180 t/day.

## Produce

1. **Name the stock and both of its flows.** Say which flow the network controls and which it does not.

2. **Produce the silo level at the end of each day**, days 1 to 12, assuming the delivery stays at the standing 180 throughout. Say on which day the silo is at its lowest, and what it is. Say on which day the burn peaks.

3. **Say on which day the silo crosses the critical level of 250 t**, and say how that day relates to the day the burn peaked.

4. **From day 8, the burn is 180 and the delivery is 180.** Say what the silo does from day 8 onward, and why. If it does not return to 700, say what would have to happen for it to.

5. **State each of the two delays, add them, and compare the sum with the length of the cold snap.** Then say what the delay cost: what would the lowest silo level have been if both delays were zero and the rule below had been running?

6. **Now apply the network's written rule** from day 1, with both delays as described. Say on which day the rule first changes the delivery, on which day the first extra tonnes arrive, and whether the lowest silo level is any different from your answer to item 2. Then say **why**, in terms of the rule rather than the people.

7. **Propose a rule keyed to the burn rather than to the silo level** — one that would have protected the critical level. State it precisely enough to be applied. Then **say what it costs**: run it, and report how many extra tonnes are ordered and how many are turned away at the gate. Compare with the written rule on both counts.

8. **Name one thing this page does not tell you that you would need in order to choose between the two rules.**
