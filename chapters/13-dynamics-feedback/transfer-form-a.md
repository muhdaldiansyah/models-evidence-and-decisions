# Chapter 13 Cold-Transfer Task — Form A

Allow about **45 minutes**.

Work from this page alone.
Do not open the chapter, the water case, the rubric, or Form B.
Every fact you need is here. Do not look anything up.

## The situation

A **district general hospital** holds a store of **platelet units** for transfusion.

Units arrive from the regional blood service on a standing daily order.
Units leave when they are transfused.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Store capacity (the agitator holds no more) | **120 units** |
| Normal holding | **100 units** |
| Critical level — below this the hospital cancels elective surgery lists | **45 units** |
| Standing daily order | **30 units/day** |
| Ordinary daily usage | **30 units/day** |

**The store held 100 units at the start of day 1.**

## Two things the hospital cannot do instantly

**The stock figure is two days old.** The store is counted each morning, but the count is reconciled against the transfusion laboratory's issue record before it is used for ordering, and reconciliation takes two days. The most recent figure any ordering decision can use describes the store as it was two days earlier.

**A change to the order takes two days to arrive.** The blood service schedules collections, and donated platelets must complete testing before release. An order changed today changes what arrives in two days' time.

## The week

A motorway collision, followed by an unrelated run of haematology admissions, raised usage for seven days.

| Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 onward |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Units transfused | 38 | 42 | 46 | 44 | 40 | 36 | 33 | **30** |

The order was left at the standing 30 units/day throughout the week, because nobody had authority to change it and the consultant haematologist was on leave.

## The hospital's written rule

The transfusion policy has contained this paragraph for six years.

> When the most recent verified stock figure is below **55 units**, set the daily order to the most recent verified usage plus **12 units**. When the most recent verified stock figure is above **85 units**, return the order to the standing 30 units/day.

## Produce

1. **Name the stock and both of its flows.** Say which flow the hospital controls and which it does not.

2. **Produce the stock at the end of each day**, days 1 to 12, assuming the order stays at the standing 30 throughout. Say on which day the store is at its lowest, and what it is. Say on which day usage peaks.

3. **Say on which day the store crosses the critical level of 45**, and say how that day relates to the day usage peaked.

4. **From day 8, usage is 30 and the order is 30.** Say what the stock does from day 8 onward, and why. If it does not return to 100, say what would have to happen for it to.

5. **State each of the two delays, add them, and compare the sum with the length of the shock.** Then say what the delay cost: what would the lowest stock have been if both delays were zero and the rule below had been running?

6. **Now apply the hospital's written rule** from day 1, with both delays as described. Say on which day the rule first changes the order, on which day the first extra units arrive, and whether the lowest stock is any different from your answer to item 2. Then say **why**, in terms of the rule rather than the people.

7. **Propose a rule keyed to usage rather than to stock** — one that would have protected the critical level. State it precisely enough to be applied. Then **say what it costs**: run it, and report how many extra units are ordered and how many are lost because the store is full. Compare with the written rule on both counts.

8. **Name one thing this page does not tell you that you would need in order to choose between the two rules.**
