## Standing rules carried forward

These were earned in earlier chapters and are not renegotiated here.

## Bounds — what was deliberately not researched

**No system dynamics methodology.** No causal loop diagram conventions, no stock-and-flow diagramming notation, no Vensim or Stella, no model-building procedure. The chapter teaches the concepts these notations encode and does not teach the notations.

**No control theory.** No transfer functions, no Laplace transforms, no Nyquist, no Bode, no PID, no controller tuning. Åström and Murray's pp. 23–24 contain on-off and proportional control and were read; the reading exists so that the chapter can use the *oscillation mechanism* on p. 24 and refuse the controller on the same page.

**No stability mathematics.** No eigenvalues, no linearization, no Lyapunov functions, no Routh–Hurwitz. The three grades of stability are taken from the prose definitions at pp. 102–103 and stated in words.

**No chaos, no bifurcation, no catastrophe.** Åström and Murray's §4.5 treats parametric and nonlocal behaviour and was not read.

**No performativity.** `perdomo2020performative` is Chapter 15's and was not extended.

**No differential equations.** The chapter's accumulation is arithmetic on a table.

## Sources considered and declined

**Forrester, *Urban Dynamics* (1969) and "Counterintuitive Behavior of Social Systems" (1971).** The origin of the policy-resistance argument. Not obtained; `sterman2002models` and `sterman2006evidence` both carry the argument with checkable pagination, and adding a third source for one claim would not strengthen it.

**Meadows, *Thinking in Systems* (2008).** A widely used popular exposition. Declined on the same grounds the book has declined popular treatments since Chapter 3: where a primary technical source is available and readable, the book uses it.

**Åström and Murray second edition.** Obtained and **declined for want of continuous printed pagination**, under standing rule 3. Recorded in the source note.

**Sterman, *Business Dynamics* (2000).** The standard textbook, and the natural home for most of this chapter's material. **Not obtained.** This is the chapter's largest gap and is recorded as such: the book teaches stocks, flows, and delays from a journal article and a test instrument rather than from the textbook that systematises them.

## Known gaps carried forward

1. **`sterman2006evidence` pp. 513–514 unread.** The article's closing sections were not needed and are not characterised.
2. **`boothsweeney2000bathtub` read at four pages of thirty-eight.** The task instruments themselves (pp. 252–261) were skimmed for structure but no claim is made about their design, and the manufacturing-case analysis at pp. 272–276 is uncharacterised.
3. **`astrom2008feedback` §4.4 Lyapunov analysis and §4.5 nonlocal behaviour unread**, deliberately.
4. **Sterman's *Business Dynamics* not obtained** — the gap above.
5. **`boothsweeney2000bathtub` is a single-institution convenience sample from 2000**, and its demographic analysis reports a gender difference as "marginally significant". The chapter uses the study's *measurements* and not its threshold verdicts, which is Chapter 8's discipline applied to the book's own source.
6. **No source was sought for the claim that overshoot is common in practice.** `sterman2006evidence` p. 508 gives the mechanism and names instances; the book does not claim a frequency.
7. The **Chapter 13 case is the water anchor's thirteenth recurrence**, and Chapter 1's Gate 1 remains open.
