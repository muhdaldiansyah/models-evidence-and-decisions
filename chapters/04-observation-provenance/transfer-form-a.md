## Form A — A city's pothole repair records

### The question being asked

The city's transport committee wants to know **which roads are in the worst condition**, in order to plan next year's resurfacing programme.

An analyst has been given the council's defect register and asked to rank roads by number of reported potholes.

### Supplied facts

- The register holds **4,180 reports** from the last two years.
- Reports arrive through two channels: **34% by telephone**, **66% through a mobile app** launched in 2023. The app requires a smartphone and a registered account.
- The council maintains most streets in the city. **Trunk roads are maintained by a national agency** and **private estate roads by their owners**; defects on either are not logged in this register.
- A report is created when a member of the public makes one. The council does not survey roads proactively.
- The app asks for a photograph. **Photo upload fails on slow mobile connections**, and the outer rural wards of the city have the poorest mobile coverage. Reports arriving without a photograph are placed in a lower-priority queue.
- Severity is recorded on a **1–5 scale**, where 5 means "immediate hazard". Defects judged more severe than 5 are still recorded as 5.
- A report is **closed 90 days after repair**, and the archived record retains only the date, the road name, and a closure code. The severity score and the photograph are not retained.
- The monthly return to the transport committee counts **defects repaired**, not defects reported.
- Some roads in the city have no residential frontage at all: an industrial estate, a bypass, and two link roads.
- Last year the council began allocating repair crews in proportion to report volume by ward. Shortly afterwards, a ward councillor organised a campaign encouraging residents to report every defect they saw.

### Produce

1. **Two processes.** Describe, in two short paragraphs, the process being asked about and — separately — the process that produced these records. Then answer in one sentence: **what was this register made for?**
2. **Stages.** Name at least three points at which the record-producing process decided what would and would not appear, and say who decided and what they were optimising.
3. **Absence.** Identify one category of road defect that **could never appear** in this register, and explain why nothing in the data reveals its absence.
4. **Relationship and size.** State whether being recorded is related to the quantity the committee cares about, and in which direction. Then say why collecting another two years of the same reports would not answer the committee's question.
5. **Out of scope.** One of the supplied facts describes something this task is *not* about — a case where the records changed because people learned they were being used. Identify it, and say how you can tell it is different in kind from the others.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form B at all.**
