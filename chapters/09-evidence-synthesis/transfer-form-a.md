# Chapter 9 Cold-Transfer Task — Form A

**You are the analyst.** Produce a written assessment for the committee.

Every fact you need is supplied. Do not look anything up; if something you need is missing, say what it is and whether it would change your answer.

Current pilot target: **45 minutes**. This is a design parameter pending pilot evidence, not a universal standard.

## The decision

A hospital trust is deciding whether to install **compliant flooring** — a shock-absorbing surface — across its inpatient wards, to reduce injurious falls.

The capital committee meets next month. Five sources have been assembled.

## The five sources

All estimates are the percentage change in **injurious falls per thousand bed-days** after installation. Negative means fewer.

| | Source | Size | Estimate |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **A** | The trust's own pilot | 2 wards | **−18%** |
| **B** | A Scandinavian multi-site study | 12 sites | **−31%** |
| **C** | The national incident-reporting database | 900 wards | **−4%** |
| **D** | A materials laboratory drop test | 8 rigs | **−52%** |
| **E** | An expert panel of six clinicians | — | **−12%** |

## Five supplied facts

**1. How the trust chose its pilot wards.** The two wards were the two with the highest recorded fall rates in the preceding year.

**2. What the Scandinavian sites are.** All twelve are **single-occupancy rooms** throughout. The trust's estate is almost entirely **multi-occupancy bays**.

**3. What the national database records.** Reporting is voluntary. There is no enforced definition of "injurious" — some trusts record any fall requiring documentation, others only those resulting in a fracture or head injury.

**4. What the drop test measures.** Impact force on an instrumented dummy dropped from bed height onto a floor panel. There is no person, no ward, and no fall — the rig measures what the surface does to an impact.

**5. What compliant flooring needs in order to work.** It reduces injury only where it is **continuously installed** across the area a person may fall onto. In the Scandinavian sites it was laid wall to wall. In the trust's bays, fixed equipment, service runs, and floor-mounted bed rails mean that no more than about **60%** of the bay floor can be covered, and the uncovered strips are along the bedsides.

**6. Two further facts about the sources.** The Scandinavian study's lead author chairs the working group that wrote the national database's incident definitions. Two of the six panel members were investigators on the trust's own pilot.

For reference: `(2 × −18 + 12 × −31 + 900 × −4 + 8 × −52) ÷ 922`.

## Produce

Write a response containing all seven items.

1. **State the quantity the committee needs**, with its population, comparison, and variable.
2. **For each of the five sources, say what quantity it is an estimate of.** Mark which are about the committee's quantity and which are not.
3. **Compute at least three ways of combining the five**, including one that weights by size. State each result and what the rule assumes.
4. **Say what the size-weighted rule does**, and why that matters here.
5. **Identify the dependencies** among the five sources, and say what they do to the appearance of agreement.
6. **Name the support factor** the effect requires, and say whether the trust's wards have it.
7. **State what would settle the question**, and for each thing you name say whether the trust could establish it this month, this year, or not at all.

Finish with **the paragraph you would put in front of the committee**: no more than five sentences, containing no single combined number.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form B at all.**
