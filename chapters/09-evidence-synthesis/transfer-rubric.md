## What a strong answer says

### On the same-question check

**Source D is not about the committee's quantity at all.** A drop rig measures impact force with no person and no fall; a laboratory benchmark scores pattern matching on synthetic streams with no customers and no ninety-second hold. Both are real measurements of something else.

**Source C's variable is not defined.** "Injurious" and "fraud loss" mean different things to different members, and 900 or 620 institutions of an undefined quantity is a large number of something.

**Source A carries a selection problem.** The pilot units were chosen as the worst, which is Chapter 7's territory and makes its estimate hard to interpret even for its own setting.

A response that reached these conclusions **before** producing a weighting has done the item.

### On the size-weighted rule

It hands **97.6%** (Form A) or **95.7%** (Form B) of the weight to the source with the undefined variable and voluntary participation.

A strong answer states the share, not just the direction, and says the obvious consequence: the size-weighted answer is source C's answer with three decorations attached.

### On dependence

Both forms supply two, and both are answerable by asking.

**Form A:** the Scandinavian study's lead author chairs the group that wrote the national database's definitions, so B and C share a measurement convention. Two of the six panel members ran the trust's pilot, so E and A are not independent.

**Form B:** the vendor sits on the consortium's data-standards committee, so B and C share definitions. Two of the four panel members ran the home-market pilot, so E and A are not independent.

**And the consequence is the scored part:** dependence makes sources agree more than independent ones would, so it raises apparent confidence exactly when it should not. Counting five sources as five pieces of evidence assumes an independence nobody checked.

### On the support factor — the item that decides the question

**Form A: continuous coverage.** Compliant flooring reduces injury only where it is laid across the area a person may fall onto. The Scandinavian sites had it wall to wall; the trust can cover about 60% of a bay, and the uncovered strips are along the bedsides — which is where someone falling out of bed lands.

**Form B: the shared registry.** The ninety-second hold is useful only where a registry can be queried in that window. The home market has one with mandatory participation. **The new market has none.**

In both, the mechanism by which the intervention works is **absent from the target setting**, and the fact is physical or institutional rather than statistical. A response offering "the settings may differ" has not done the item.

### On what would settle it

Strong answers name things like:

- **one comparable case** — a multi-occupancy ward with partial coverage; an institution operating the rule without a registry;
- **an argument from mechanism** — what partial coverage does to impact energy; what a nightly batch does to a ninety-second hold;
- **using the unsuitable source for what it can do** — the drop rig can quantify what the surface does per impact, which is one term in the mechanism argument; the laboratory benchmark can quantify detection rate independent of the hold;
- **arranging one case deliberately** — one bay, one branch, chosen for reasons unrelated to how bad it is.

**Full marks require costing them.** At least one of the items above needs no new data and could be done this month.

### On the committee paragraph

It must contain no single combined number. A response that ends with "approximately −20%" has reproduced the failure in a shorter form.

Acceptable shape: the range and what drives it; which sources are about a different quantity; the support factor and whether the target setting has it; one thing that could be established quickly.

## Three answers that look right and are not

**"We pooled the sources for a combined n of 922."** Adding sample sizes across sources that measure different quantities produces a large number attached to nothing.

**"Four of five agree that the effect is negative, so it works."** They share definitions and people, and one of them has no customers in it.

**"The effect is well established, so we should expect it here unless there is evidence otherwise."** This makes transport the default. The support factor is absent from the target setting and that is evidence otherwise, sitting in fact 5.

## A note on tone

Two dimensions — running the same-question check before weighting, and naming a physical support factor — are the ones this chapter exists to install, and they are the ones most often scored 0 by readers whose arithmetic is perfect.
