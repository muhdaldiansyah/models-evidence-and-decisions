## A third rule, adopted here

> **Cite the version whose pagination you can see.**

This chapter's research obtained the standard transportability paper — Pearl and Bareinboim, "External Validity: From do-calculus to Transportability across Populations", forthcoming in *Statistical Science* — only as UCLA technical report R-400, last revised May 2014, which carries no printed page numbers at all. Its pages are stamped only with a LaTeX build footer.

**It is therefore not cited anywhere in this book**, despite being the obvious source for §5, because a locator that cannot be checked by a reader is not a locator.

What is cited instead is `bareinboim2016fusion`, obtained as the authors' PNAS reprint, which carries the published pagination 7345–7352 on its pages and covers transportability as one of four fusion tasks.

## What this plan does not attempt

- **Any synthesis method.** No inverse-variance weighting, no random-effects models, no heterogeneity statistics. The chapter demonstrates that weighting rules disagree; teaching one would be teaching the rule whose failure is the demonstration.
- **Selection diagrams or the transport formula.** `bareinboim2016fusion` develops both; both require the graphical machinery Chapter 7 declined.
- **Publication bias and funnel plots.** Named at most.
- **Elicitation protocols for expert judgment.** Chapter 6 already taught scoring; this chapter reuses it.
- **The replication-crisis literature as a literature.** Chapter 8 declined to characterise it and this chapter does not reopen that.
- **Bayesian evidence combination.** Chapter 6 declined the underlying argument.
- **INUS causality as a topic.** `deaton2016rct` p. 28 names it, attributing it to Mackie (1974). The book takes the support-factor idea and not the analysis.
