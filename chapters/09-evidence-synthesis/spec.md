## Central question

What do many imperfect sources jointly support — here?

## Core competence

Reason about heterogeneous and dependent evidence, replication, evidence synthesis, expert judgment, external validity, generalizability, target populations, and transportability at an appropriate conceptual level.

## Role in the book

Chapter 9 closes Part II. It is the chapter that stops a reader who has learned to interrogate one body of evidence from assuming that five bodies of evidence are five times better.

**The em dash in the central question is the chapter.** *What do many imperfect sources jointly support — here?* The first clause is synthesis; the word after the dash is transport; and the second is what makes the first hard.

Seven chapters defer here — more than to any chapter except Chapter 7 — and the debts are itemised in `readiness-audit.md` §3.

Two of its three principal sources come due rather than needing to be found. `deaton2016rct` §2 was twice recorded as unread and belonging here. `meng2018paradox` insight (IV) was recorded in Chapter 4 and left unused because Chapter 4 concerned one dataset.

## Exercise architecture

1. **Opening attempt (§1).** Which of five reports would you trust, and how would you combine them? Six minutes, unscored.
2. **Same question (§2).** Attribute-by-attribute, which sources are about the utility's quantity?
3. **Four rules (§3).** Compute all four; say what each assumes.
4. **Dependence (§3).** Which of the five are not independent, and how would you find out?
5. **Replication (§4).** What would a sixth confirming source add?
6. **Transport (§5).** What would have to be true for source B to apply at Hillcrest?
7. **Planted-defect diagnosis (§6).** Five defects.
8. **Cold transfer (§7).** One assigned parallel form.
9. **Retrieval and delayed retest (§7).**

### Planted defects

| Planted defect | Collapse targeted |
|---|---|
| "We pooled all five studies, giving us 1,461 observations" | combining means adding sample sizes |
| "Four of the five agree, so the finding is robust" | agreement among dependent sources is evidence |
| "It has been replicated three times, so it will hold here" | replication settles transport |
| "The trial was internally valid, so the effect is real and applies" | causality settles generalizability |
| "There's no evidence it works differently here, so we'll assume it transfers" | transport is the default |

### Rubric dimensions

1. Sources checked for being about the same quantity before any combining.
2. More than one weighting rule produced.
3. Size rejected as a weight, with a reason.
4. A dependence between sources identified.
5. Replication's limits stated in both directions.
6. A support factor named that is physical, not statistical.
7. A verdict that says what would have to be true here.

## Transfer target

> Given several sources bearing on one decision, with sizes, estimates, and stated defects, determine which are about the same quantity, produce at least two weighting rules and their answers, identify a dependence, name the support factor that decides whether any of them transfers, and state what would have to be true of the target setting.

### Parallel forms

- **Form A — a hospital trust choosing a fall-prevention flooring** (physical/technical).
- **Form B — a bank choosing a fraud-screening rule for a new market** (institutional).

Both supply: five sources of unequal size and quality; a largest-and-worst source; at least two sources about a different quantity; a dependence between two sources; and a support factor absent from the target setting.

Every prior transfer and contrast domain is excluded. Neither domain is sensitive.

Chapter 9 must not claim durable far transfer.

8. Establishing causality settles generalizability.

10. Expert judgment is not evidence.
