## Form A — Indoor air quality in a school

### Situation

A secondary school of 18 classrooms is deciding two things this term.

- **Purpose 1.** Report to the school board whether the building's ventilation meets the authority's air-quality standard.
- **Purpose 2.** Decide which rooms should receive mechanical ventilation in the summer works programme, given a budget that covers four rooms.

### The current arrangement

The authority's standard requires that **"air quality is adequate for occupancy."** It does not define the phrase.

The school's facilities manager operates to a written procedure:

> Air quality is adequate when the corridor carbon-dioxide reading stays below **1,000 ppm** during teaching hours.

Carbon dioxide is used because people exhale it, so its concentration rises when a space is occupied and under-ventilated. The 1,000 ppm figure is the school's own working figure.

### Supplied facts

- The school has **one** fixed carbon-dioxide sensor, mounted in the **main corridor**. It was installed there because the corridor had a power outlet and the classrooms did not.
- The corridor sensor logs **once per hour**.
- Sensor display resolution: **1 ppm**. Repeatability: **±15 ppm**. At its last service check the sensor was found to be reading **80 ppm low**.
- Corridor reading at 11:00 on a recent teaching day: **780 ppm**.
- On the same morning, a portable meter was carried into two rooms:
  - **Room 12** — 30 pupils, door and window closed: **1,640 ppm**
  - **Room 3** — 12 pupils, window open: **520 ppm**
- The portable meter is checked against a reference gas cylinder before each use.
- Carbon dioxide is produced by the occupants. It is **not** produced by cleaning solvents, printer toner, or particulates blown in from the road outside.
- The road outside carries heavy traffic at the start and end of the school day.
- Classroom occupancy varies from 11 to 32 pupils. The corridor is occupied only between lessons.
- No record is kept of which windows are open.

### Produce

1. **The ladder.** State the construct, then write a working definition that would serve **Purpose 2**. Say explicitly what your definition decides about **where**, **when**, and **how much**.
2. **Disagreement.** Using the supplied readings, show that the school's current working definition and yours give different answers. State the disagreement as numbers.
3. **The interpretation.** Write, in one sentence, what would have to be true for the corridor readings to support the claim "this building's air quality is adequate". Then name one observation that would count against it.
4. **The offset.** Identify one systematic offset in the supplied measurements. State why taking more readings from the same instrument would not remove it.
5. **The proxy, and the line.** Name what carbon dioxide is standing in for, and one circumstance in this school where the substitution would break. Then place each item below on the line between *the number is wrong* and *the wrong numbers exist*:
   - the corridor sensor reads 80 ppm low;
   - there is no sensor in any classroom;
   - "adequate" was defined as below 1,000 ppm in the corridor;
   - the sensor was installed in the corridor because that is where the power outlet was.

**Stop when your response is complete. Do not open the rubric until then, and do not open Form B at all.**
