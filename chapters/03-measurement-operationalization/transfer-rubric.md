# Chapter 3 — Cold-Transfer Rubric

Open this file only **after** completing the transfer response.

Score each dimension from **0 to 2**. Use the dimensions diagnostically; there is **no validated aggregate cut score**.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construct | Stated as an instrument or a number ("air quality is what the sensor reads") | Stated as a thing, but too loose to constrain anything | Stated as a thing, distinct from any procedure for measuring it |
| Working definition | Absent, or restates the construct | Written, but leaves one of where / when / how much unspecified | All three specified, and what the definition **decided** is named |
| Disagreement | Not computed | Asserted that the definitions differ | Two operationalizations produced and their disagreement stated **as numbers** |
| Interpretation as a claim | Scores treated as self-evidently meaning the construct | An interpretation is stated | Interpretation stated **and** an observation named that would count against it |
| Validity predicated correctly | Writes that an instrument is or is not valid | Avoids the error without stating the alternative | Locates validity in the interpretation of scores for a use |
| Reliability vs validity | Treats consistency as correctness | Distinguishes them abstractly | Identifies a case in the supplied facts that is consistent and wrong |
| Systematic offset | Not identified, or identified and called noise | Identified as systematic | Identified, **and** repetition explicitly ruled out as a remedy, with the reason |
| Proxy | Not identified, or treated as equivalent to the construct | Named as a proxy | Named, with the specific circumstances under which the substitution breaks |

Two further checks, scored pass or fail rather than 0–2:

- **Boundary placement.** Were the four items placed, and was the "why is the instrument there" item recognised as genuinely mixed rather than forced onto one side?
- **Supplied facts respected.** Were any numbers invented that the form did not supply? Saying "this is missing and it would change my answer" is a strong response; filling the gap is a category error.

Regardless of any total, major category errors include:

- writing that an instrument "is valid" or "has been validated";
- proposing more measurements as the remedy for a systematic offset;
- treating a precision or resolution figure as evidence of correctness;
- treating the current working definition as the meaning of the construct;
- comparing two scores produced under different working definitions as though they were commensurable;
- inventing a fact the form did not supply;
- forcing the mixed boundary item onto one side without noticing it is mixed.
