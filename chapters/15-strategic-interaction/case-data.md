# Chapter 15 Case Data

Status: frozen case facts. The manuscript may not contradict this file.

**The water anchor's fifteenth recurrence, and the first with a second party.** No new physical fact about the network. Every figure was computed and checked before drafting.

## 1. The metric, and the date

The regulator has always collected **properties below minimum pressure**, counted at a **representative measurement point** in each of the utility's fifteen zones — the arrangement Chapter 3 established.

**In 2019 the count became an incentive.** It entered a published comparative table with a financial consequence attached, and **£1.8m** was at stake for this utility across the price-control period. Before 2019 it was reported and unpenalised.

**That date is documentary**, not inferred. It is in the regulator's published determination.

## 2. The series

| Year | Properties reported below minimum pressure | Low-pressure complaints | Complaints per reported property |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2016 | 1,280 | 875 | 0.68 |
| 2017 | 1,210 | 884 | 0.73 |
| 2018 | 1,230 | 911 | 0.74 |
| **2019** | **770** | **905** | **1.18** |
| 2020 | 690 | 880 | 1.28 |
| 2021 | 640 | 915 | 1.43 |
| 2022 | 610 | 930 | 1.52 |

**Pre-2019 mean: 1,240 reported, 890 complaints, ratio 0.72.**

| Comparison | Reported count | Complaints |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019 against the pre-2019 mean | **−37.9%** | **+1.7%** |
| 2022 against the pre-2019 mean | **−50.8%** | **+4.5%** |

## 3. What actually happened

**No capital work was done. Nothing in the network changed.**

Between 2018 and 2020 the utility re-designated the representative measurement point in **9 of its 15 zones**. **Seven of the nine moved to a point at lower elevation**, where pressure is higher.

**Every move was documented, justified in writing, and compliant with the regulator's guidance**, which delegates measurement-point selection to the licensee.

**Nothing in this case is illegal, and the manuscript must make that impossible to miss.**

## 4. The relationship that broke, quantified

The utility's planning model used **0.72 complaints per reported property**, fitted on 2016–2018 and stable across those three years.

**Applied to 2022's reported count of 610, it forecasts 439 complaints.**

**The actual figure was 930.**

**The outcome is 112% higher than the forecast** — or, put the other way, the forecast is **53% below what happened** — on a relationship that had held for years, broken by nothing that happened to the network.

The gap is **491 complaints**.

## 5. The four mechanisms on this case

| Mechanism | On this case | Needs an agent? |
|---|---|---|
| **Regressional** | Zones enter the count by measured pressure at one point, which contains noise. Present before 2019. | no |
| **Extremal** | Pushing the count down moves the utility into a region — sensors at the most favourable points — where the old count-to-complaints relationship describes nothing. | no |
| **Causal** | Moving a sensor intervenes on the proxy and not on the goal. Nobody's pressure changed. | the regulator's action |
| **Adversarial** | The utility selected a measurement protocol knowing which protocol the regulator would score. | yes |

**All four are present**, which the source says is usual. **Only the fourth involves anybody deciding anything.**

## 6. The strategic structure

Payoffs are **the book's own construction**, not derived from any source.

Units: the regulator's payoff is in **hundreds of properties genuinely receiving adequate pressure**; the utility's is in **£m, net of its costs**.

| | Utility: report as sited | Utility: re-site to favourable points |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulator: no incentive** | 12.4 , 0 | 12.4 , −0.3 |
| **Regulator: incentive on the count** | 13.1 , −1.8 | **12.4 , +1.8** |

**The bottom-right cell is the equilibrium.**

Given the incentive, the utility does better re-siting (+1.8 against −1.8).
Given re-siting, the regulator does no worse keeping the incentive than dropping it (12.4 either way).
**Neither party can improve by changing its own action alone.**

**And it delivers exactly what the top-left cell delivered** — 12.4 — at a cost of £1.8m transferred and nine measurement points moved.

**The equilibrium is no better than the arrangement before anybody optimised anything, and it cost £1.8m to reach.**

**Note the cell that would have been best for the regulator: 13.1, top-right of the incentive row** — and it is unreachable, because it requires the utility to accept −1.8 when +1.8 is available.

## 7. The six machinery terms, one instance each

| Term | Instance |
|---|---|
| Strategic dependence | The utility's best protocol depends on what the regulator scores. |
| Incentive | The £1.8m, which changed no physical fact and changed behaviour. |
| Equilibrium as consistency | The bottom-right cell; both parties' expectations about the other are correct. |
| Commitment | The regulator could bind itself to fixed measurement points for five years — removing the response, and its own ability to correct genuinely bad siting. |
| Information asymmetry | The utility knows which points are favourable; the regulator does not, and cannot cheaply find out. |
| Delegation | The regulator's guidance delegates point selection to the licensee, which makes the asymmetry actionable. |

**`principal-agent` and `information asymmetry` are unsourced** — see `../../decisions/0022` clause 9.

## 8. What this case may not be used for

- Any claim about a real regulator, a real water company, or any real incentive scheme. **The case is synthetic.**
- Any suggestion that measurement-point re-designation is common, or that regulated companies generally behave this way. **No frequency is claimed.**
- Any claim that the payoff numbers were derived from anything. They are constructed to make the strategic structure legible and nothing more.
- Re-estimating anything Chapter 7 declared not identified.
- Any implication of dishonesty. Every action in the case is legal, documented, and compliant.
