## The situation

A **county police force** records the outcome of every reported crime. A case recorded as **cleared** is one closed with a named suspect and a disposal — a charge, a caution, a community resolution, or an out-of-court disposal.

**Cases cleared** is the force's headline detection measure.

**In 2019 it entered a published comparative table** covering all forces in the region, with a share of a discretionary funding allocation attached to relative performance. The commencement date is in the funding body's published framework. Before 2019 the figure was collected and reported and nothing followed from it.

## The series

| Year | Cases recorded as cleared | Convictions secured at court |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2016 | 3,120 | 1,560 |
| 2017 | 3,100 | 1,530 |
| 2018 | 3,060 | 1,545 |
| 2019 | 3,910 | 1,540 |
| 2020 | 4,180 | 1,505 |
| 2021 | 4,300 | 1,555 |
| 2022 | 4,460 | 1,520 |

**Recorded crime over the period was flat**, between 10,180 and 10,480 a year.

**Officer numbers fell 4% over the period.** No new investigative unit was created, and no new technology was deployed.

## What the force did

Between 2018 and 2020 the force's crime-recording unit reissued its internal guidance on **which disposal codes count as a clearance**.

The revised guidance brought **community resolutions** and two categories of **out-of-court disposal** into routine use as clearances, where previously local practice had recorded them inconsistently.

**Every code used is in the national recording standard. The guidance was documented, circulated, and available to the inspectorate.** Nothing was concealed and no case was misrecorded against the standard.

## What the force relies on

The force's **court liaison team** forecasts its file-preparation workload using a ratio fitted on 2016–2018: **0.50 convictions per cleared case**. The ratio was stable across those three years and is used to set the team's establishment.

## A decision on the table

The force is deciding whether to fund a **£4,600,000** investigative-capacity programme over three years, on the basis that clearances have risen and the constraint is now downstream.

An independent **case-file audit** has been quoted at **£210,000**. It would establish, for a sample of cleared cases, what proportion involved an investigation of the kind the programme would expand.

3. **Four mechanisms can make a metric come apart from the goal behind it: one from selecting on a noisy proxy, one from pushing the metric to an extreme, one from an action that changes the measurement rather than the thing measured, and one from a party who knows what is being scored.** Say which are present here, and which would still be present if the force had changed nothing at all.

4. **Quantify the broken relationship.** Use the 0.50 ratio to forecast 2022 convictions from 2022 clearances. Compare with the actual figure. State the error as a percentage and say who bears its consequences.

5. **Describe the situation as an interaction between two parties.** Name them, name two actions each could take, and say which combination is stable — an outcome where neither could do better by changing its own action alone. Say whether that outcome is better or worse than the arrangement before 2019, and for whom.

6. **Say what is wrong with each of these remedies**, in one sentence each: replace the measure with a better one; add three more measures; audit the disposal codes.

7. **Decide on the case-file audit.** Say what it would resolve, what it costs relative to the decision it informs, and what you recommend. If you cannot compute what it is worth, say why not.
