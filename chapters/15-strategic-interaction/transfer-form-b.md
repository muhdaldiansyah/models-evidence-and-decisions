# Chapter 15 Cold-Transfer Task — Form B

Allow about **50 minutes**.

Work from this page alone.
Do not open the chapter, the water case, the rubric, or Form A.
Every fact you need is here. Do not look anything up.

**Everything described below is lawful and was disclosed.** No individual is described and no real college is depicted.

---

## The situation

A **further-education college** records, for every enrolled learner, whether they complete their course.

**Learners recorded as completing** is the college's headline achievement measure.

**In 2019 it entered the funding formula.** A share of the college's grant became conditional on completion performance against a national benchmark. The commencement date is in the funding body's published rules. Before 2019 the figure was collected and reported and nothing followed from it.

## The series

| Year | Learners recorded as completing | Learners in employment or further study six months after leaving |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2016 | 2,180 | 1,840 |
| 2017 | 2,150 | 1,865 |
| 2018 | 2,205 | 1,820 |
| 2019 | 2,680 | 1,835 |
| 2020 | 2,810 | 1,795 |
| 2021 | 2,895 | 1,850 |
| 2022 | 2,960 | 1,810 |

**Total enrolments over the period were flat**, between 3,040 and 3,110 a year.

**Teaching staff numbers fell 3% over the period.** No curriculum was redesigned and no new teaching method was introduced.

## What the college did

Between 2018 and 2020 the college changed **when a learner counts as being in the cohort**.

The funding body's rules set a census date after which a learner is counted; withdrawals before that date are not. The college moved its census date later in the year, and tightened the administrative processing of early withdrawals so that they are recorded promptly.

**Both changes are within the funding body's published rules. The change was documented, notified, and available to the inspectorate.** Nothing was concealed and no learner's record was falsified.

## What the college relies on

The college's **careers service** forecasts its advisory caseload using a ratio fitted on 2016–2018: **0.85 learners entering employment or further study per recorded completion**. The ratio was stable across those three years and is used to set the service's establishment.

## A decision on the table

The college is deciding whether to commit **£3,100,000** over four years to expanding its most successful curriculum areas, identified as those whose recorded completion rates have risen most since 2018.

An independent **destination survey and enrolment audit** has been quoted at **£140,000**. It would establish, for a sample of learners, what happened to those who left before the census date.

## Produce

1. **Say what happened**, in three sentences, using the two columns and the fact that enrolments were flat.

2. **Apply the discriminator.** State the date the measure acquired consequences, say where you got it, and say what you find at that date and in the years before it. Then say what your finding does and does not establish.

3. **Four mechanisms can make a metric come apart from the goal behind it: one from selecting on a noisy proxy, one from pushing the metric to an extreme, one from an action that changes the measurement rather than the thing measured, and one from a party who knows what is being scored.** Say which are present here, and which would still be present if the college had changed nothing at all.

4. **Quantify the broken relationship.** Use the 0.85 ratio to forecast 2022 destinations from 2022 completions. Compare with the actual figure. State the error as a percentage and say who bears its consequences.

5. **Describe the situation as an interaction between two parties.** Name them, name two actions each could take, and say which combination is stable — an outcome where neither could do better by changing its own action alone. Say whether that outcome is better or worse than the arrangement before 2019, and for whom.

6. **Say what is wrong with each of these remedies**, in one sentence each: replace the measure with a better one; add three more measures; audit the census-date change.

7. **Decide on the destination survey.** Say what it would resolve, what it costs relative to the decision it informs, and what you recommend. If you cannot compute what it is worth, say why not.

8. **Name one thing this page does not tell you that would change your answer to item 5.**

9. **One group of people appears in this case and in none of its numbers.** Name them, and say what that omission does to items 1 and 4.
