## The situation

A social landlord manages around 4,000 homes.

Reports of damp from one block, **Marlow Court**, have risen sharply this quarter. Two explanations are on the table.

> **Explanation R — the damp itself has increased.**
> Something in the building fabric has changed, so more homes genuinely have damp than did before.

> **Explanation S — the reporting has increased.**
> The condition is roughly what it was, and a change in how residents report has brought existing damp to the surface. A tenant app was introduced across the stock four months ago.

Both are consistent with the rise in reports. They point at different responses — a fabric survey and works programme, or a change in how the reporting figures are read — and at very different costs.

## The register

The landlord keeps a register of past investigations into sudden rises in reports, across all report types and all blocks:

| Investigation outcome | Count |
|---|---:|
| A real change in condition | **7** |
| A change in reporting | **5** |
| **Total** | **12** |

The register covers nine years. Three of the twelve predate the current housing management system and were transcribed by hand.

## The proposed observation

Survey a random sample of **30 Marlow Court homes that have not reported damp**, and record moisture readings against the landlord's threshold.

The observation of interest is **more than 6 of the 30 reading above threshold**.

The surveying team supplies what to expect:

| | More than 6 of 30 above threshold | 6 or fewer |
|---|---:|---:|
| If Explanation R holds | **0.75** | 0.25 |
| If Explanation S holds | **0.20** | 0.80 |

The survey costs about a week of two surveyors' time and requires access appointments with 30 households.

## A further detail

The housing officer, asked for anything else, adds:

> *The rise began in the same month as the first cold snap of the year.*

The surveying team's assessment of that report:

| | Rise coincides with the first cold snap |
|---|---:|
| If Explanation R holds | **0.70** |
| If Explanation S holds | **0.65** |

## The team's forecasting record

For every block referred for investigation, the housing team states the chance that **works will be needed**. Over the last 36 referrals:

| Stated probability | Referrals | Works needed | Observed frequency |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 80% | 12 | 6 | |
| 60% | 12 | 7 | |
| 40% | 12 | 5 | |
| **Total** | **36** | **18** | |

## Produce

Write a response containing all six items.

1. **State a probability for Explanation R before the survey**, and write what it is conditional on. Name the population your number comes from.
2. **Work out what the survey is worth.** Compute the ratio between the two supplied numbers and say in words what it means.
3. **Work both branches.** What should the landlord believe if more than 6 read above threshold? If 6 or fewer do? Give both as probabilities.
4. **The cold snap detail.** Compute what it does to your number, and say what follows about acting on it.
5. **The forecasting record.** Complete the observed column. State the pattern in one sentence a director could act on, and say what a team that stated 50% for every referral would score.
6. **Should the landlord run the survey?** One paragraph, using your own numbers.
