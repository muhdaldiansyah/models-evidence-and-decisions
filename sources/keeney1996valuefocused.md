## Role in Chapter 1

This source supports the introductory decision-framing principle that an analyst should not treat the initially supplied alternatives as a complete or privileged option set.

The inspected abstract states that conventional approaches often focus first on alternatives, while value-focused thinking begins with values because alternatives are means for achieving what matters. It explicitly describes creating better alternatives, identifying decision opportunities, and using fundamental values to guide decision-making activities.

Chapter 1 uses only the minimal implication needed for first-pass reasoning:
- ask whether the supplied alternatives are artificially narrow;
- consider a plausible additional, combined, contingent, information-gathering, or escalation alternative when material;
- keep systematic value structuring and alternative-generation methods in Chapter 10.
