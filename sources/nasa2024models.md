# `nasa2024models`

## Bibliographic record

National Aeronautics and Space Administration. 2024. *NASA-STD-7009B: Standard for Models and Simulations*. Version B. Approved 5 March 2024.

Stable page: https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/nasa/nasa-std-7009

## Verification status

Verified against the relevant pages of the standard during the Chapter 1 intended-use research task.

## Locators and direct support

- §3, `Intended Use`: establishes `intended use` as the expected purpose and application of a model or simulation.
- §4.1: places intended use at the beginning of the M&S lifecycle requirements.
- §4.1.1.1 [M&S 40], file pp. 20–21: requires the intended use to be documented, including what is represented, expected results, intended validation domain, and decisions the results will inform.
- §4.1.1.5 [M&S 43], file pp. 20–21: ties acceptance criteria to verification, validation, uncertainty, sensitivity, and other technical criteria used to judge whether the M&S satisfies its intended use.

## Chapter 1 use

Direct support for `intended use` as established modeling-and-simulation terminology and for the proposition that overall acceptance or adequacy must be judged relative to a stated use.

## Author synthesis / caution

The book extends the question of `intended use` to analyses, estimates, forecasts, and recommendations as a pedagogical synthesis.
NASA-STD-7009B should not be cited as establishing that broader cross-disciplinary definition.

### Verified locators

- p. 18, on the form the requirements take: "maintenance of the record implies that the outcome or product is re-established as a result of any changes to either the RWS or the M&S."
- p. 30, §4.3 M&S Use: the use phase "begins with a specific use being proposed and the assessment of the proposed use against the M&S' permissible uses."
- p. 39, requirement **[M&S 41]**: "A plan for the acquisition, development, operation, maintenance, and retirement of the M&S (including identifying the responsible organizations) shall be maintained."
- p. 86, Appendix F: "The life cycle of a model or simulation, like that of any system, has two general parts: M&S development, which includes M&S initiation, concept development, M&S design, M&S construction, and M&S testing; and M&S application, which includes use (or operation) and M&S archiving (including the associated artifacts, products, and analysis performed during a specific use)."
- p. 87: "This testing identifies the M&S' limits of operation, i.e., where the M&S is known to work correctly (i.e., verified and validated). At the end of M&S testing, the M&S' capabilities, assumptions, and limits of operation are recorded and assessed with respect to acceptance criteria [M&S 43] to determine the permissible uses of the M&S."
- p. 87: "Once M&S testing is successfully completed, the M&S is released, along with guidance of the M&S' capabilities and domain of permissible use, ending M&S development."
- p. 87: "During the use (or operations) phase, the M&S may or may not be used by those who developed it. In both cases, and especially the latter case, the use of an M&S starts with an assessment of whether or not the proposed use of the M&S sufficiently matches the permissible use."
- p. 87, **the sentence Chapter 17 turns on**: "Each application of the M&S restarts the M&S use/operation with an assessment of permissible uses against the needs of that specific proposed use."
- p. 87: "If the proposed M&S use does not meet the defined permissible use, the proposed use will either be rejected or possibly allowed with the appropriate restrictions, caveats, or placarding required."
- p. 88, on the life cycle's shape: "The classic waterfall life cycle is idealized as a linear flow, though reverse-flow loops to previous phases are possible (even expected) if problems in M&S development or use occur."
- p. 12, definition: "Credibility: The quality to elicit belief or trust in M&S results."

### Chapter 17 cautions

- **`permissible use` is a property of a pairing** — a model with a proposed application — not of the model. This is the fourth appearance in this book of the relation-not-property shape, after `validity`, `transportability`, and `observability`.
- **Read at seven pages of eighty-eight.** Nothing is claimed about the standard's credibility assessment scales (Appendix E), its criticality assessment (Appendix D), its assurance architecture, its tailoring provisions, or its waiver process, all of which were seen and none of which was read.
- **The development requirements of §4.2 belong to Chapters 2 to 8** and are not taken here.
- **Nothing about NASA, spaceflight, or programme management is claimed.** The book uses the standard's life-cycle account across domains, and that widening is the book's own pedagogical synthesis, as Chapter 1's note already records for `intended use`.
- **`retirement` is used because the standard requires a plan for it.** The book has not mentioned model retirement in sixteen chapters, and the manuscript says so.
