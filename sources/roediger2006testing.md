# Source Note: roediger2006testing

## Citation

Henry L. Roediger III and Jeffrey D. Karpicke. 2006. “Test-Enhanced Learning: Taking Memory Tests Improves Long-Term Retention.” *Psychological Science* 17(3): 249–255. DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x.

## Verification

Verified against the publisher article record and abstract.

## Role in Chapter 1

Load-bearing support for retrieval from memory and delayed practice rather than ending the chapter with rereading.

The verified abstract reports that repeated study performed better on an immediate five-minute test, while prior testing produced better retention on delayed tests at two days and one week.

Chapter 1 uses the narrow implication that the reader should reconstruct the navigation structure from memory and later attempt the reasoning again after a delay.

## Cautions

- The study does not establish that 7–14 days is the optimal interval for this book.
- The study does not establish that retrieval of a complex reasoning architecture behaves identically to retrieval of prose material.
- The exact retest schedule remains a pilot parameter.
