# Source Note: sweller1985worked

## Citation

John Sweller and Graham A. Cooper. 1985. “The Use of Worked Examples as a Substitute for Problem Solving in Learning Algebra.” *Cognition and Instruction* 2(1): 59–89. DOI: 10.1207/s1532690xci0201_3.

## Verification

Verified against the publisher abstract and an author/institutional publication record.

## Role in Chapter 1

Support for showing novices a complete worked performance before requiring full independent production.

The verified abstract reports five experiments and, in the relevant comparisons, faster processing and subsequent solution with fewer mathematical errors for learners studying worked examples relative to conventional problem solving on structurally similar material.

Chapter 1 uses only the narrow instructional implication that a novice should see the complete reasoning performance before the full cold-transfer task.

## Cautions

- The direct experiments concern algebra, not interdisciplinary reasoning under uncertainty.
- Benefits reported in the abstract are tied to structurally similar subsequent problems; do not use this source to promise far transfer.
- The water-supply worked case remains a book-specific pedagogical design, not a direct replication of these experiments.
