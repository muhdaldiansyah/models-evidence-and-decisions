## Verified locators

- **p. 10**: "This was Fisher's innovation: not that randomization balanced other causes between treatments and controls but that, conditional on our caveat above, randomization provides the basis for calculating the size of the error."
- **p. 10**: "Given the absence of treatment-related post-randomization changes in other causes, randomization yields an unbiased estimate of the ATE in the trial sample as well as a sound method for measuring error of estimation in that sample; therein lies its virtue, not that it yields precise estimates through balance."

## Role in Chapter 7

- supplies what randomization does buy — an unbiased estimate in the trial sample and a basis for computing the error — and what it does not, namely balance in any particular trial;
- supplies a **documented, published** overstatement rather than a straw man, which is what makes the correction land;
- supplies the sample-size observation that connects this chapter's point to Chapter 6's argument that a single instance cannot exhibit a property defined over an ensemble;
- supplies, at p. 8, the one-sentence route forward to Chapter 9 on extending results beyond the trial sample.

## Cautions

**The quoted manuals are quoted, not read.** The Inter-American Development Bank / World Bank manual, and the other three sources quoted in §1.2, were not obtained. The manuscript cites them **as reported at** `deaton2016rct` p. 10 and never directly. This is the same handling Chapter 6 gave Brier (1950) via `gneiting2007scoring`.

**"Generally inferior to good control" is the authors' position in a contested debate.** The manuscript must attribute it rather than assert it. The paper is a well-known critique and its claims are argued against by serious people; a book that presented its conclusions as consensus would be misreporting the field.

**Do not use this source to argue that randomized trials are unreliable.** The paper's own abstract says RCTs "would be more useful if there were more realistic expectations of them" — a call for accurate expectations, not for abandonment. A chapter that used it as ammunition against experiments would be quoting against the source's stated purpose.

**Only pp. 7–11 and pp. 26–29 support anything here.** The paper's treatment of extrapolation, of Bayesian updating from trial results, and of evidence hierarchies is unread and uncharacterised.

**Chapter 9 uses pp. 27–29** for replication in both directions, `external validity` as a criticised concept, `support factor`, and the condition under which two populations share an average effect. Russell (1912), Mackie (1974), and Cartwright and Hardie (2012) are cited **within** those pages and were not obtained; the manuscript uses them **as reported at** this source and claims nothing about them.
