# Source Note: atkinson2003fading

## Citation

Robert K. Atkinson, Alexander Renkl, and Mary Margaret Merrill. 2003. “Transitioning From Studying Examples to Solving Problems: Effects of Self-Explanation Prompts and Fading Worked-Out Steps.” *Journal of Educational Psychology* 95(4): 774–783. DOI: 10.1037/0022-0663.95.4.774.

## Verification

Verified against an institutional research record carrying the article metadata and original abstract.

## Role in Chapter 1

Load-bearing support for combining progressively reduced worked-example support with prompts that direct attention to underlying principles.

The verified abstract reports two experiments in which fading worked-out steps combined with self-explanation prompts produced benefits on near and far transfer in the studied domain without additional time on task.

Chapter 1 uses the bounded implication that support should be reduced progressively rather than removed all at once, and that fading should be paired with principle-focused explanation.

## Cautions

- Do not claim that the study validates this book's exact complete-example → faded-case → cold-transfer sequence.
- Do not remove domain facts required for reasoning; fade reasoning support, not necessary case information.
- Exact fading direction, number of stages, and prompt count remain book-specific design choices to pilot.
