# Source note: `shmueli2010predict`

## Citation

Shmueli, Galit. 2010. “To Explain or to Predict?” *Statistical Science* 25(3): 289–310. DOI 10.1214/10-STS330.

## Verification status

Verified direct source for the Chapter 1 prediction distinction at introductory depth.

The article was inspected in the author-posted arXiv version, which reproduces the *Statistical Science* publication metadata and DOI.

## Role in Chapter 1

- supports treating prediction as an analytical aim distinct from description and causal explanation;
- supports defining predictive modeling around new or future observations;
- supports the point that temporal forecasting is a future-directed form of prediction;
- supports the warning that a predictive relationship may capture association rather than a causal relationship;
- supports the warning that a useful predictor is not automatically a causal lever.

## Verified locators

- §1.2, printed p. 3: predictive modeling is defined as using a statistical model or data-mining algorithm to predict new or future observations; the definition includes temporal forecasting.
- §1.3, printed p. 3: descriptive modeling is distinguished from prediction and may use regression to capture association rather than causal inference or prediction.
- §1.5, printed pp. 4–7, especially printed p. 5: the predictive/explanatory distinction includes a causation–association contrast; predictive modeling can capture association between inputs and outcomes without treating the relationship as causal.

## Chapter 1 use and cautions

Chapter 1 does not import Shmueli's entire explanatory-versus-predictive framework as a universal taxonomy. The article is used for the narrower claim that prediction of new/future observables is a distinct aim and can rely on associations that need not have causal interpretation.

Formal predictive distributions, forecast uncertainty, scoring, calibration, and out-of-sample evaluation remain Chapter 6 and Chapter 8 material.
