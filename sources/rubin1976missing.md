## Citation

Rubin, Donald B. 1976. "Inference and Missing Data." *Biometrika* 63 (3): 581–592. DOI 10.1093/biomet/63.3.581.

## Verified content — the published summary, verbatim

> "When making sampling distribution inferences about the parameter of the data, θ, it is appropriate to ignore the process that causes missing data if the missing data are 'missing at random' and the observed data are 'observed at random', but these inferences are generally conditional on the observed pattern of missing data. When making direct-likelihood or Bayesian inferences about θ, it is appropriate to ignore the process that causes missing data if the missing data are missing at random and the parameter of the missing data process is 'distinct' from θ. These conditions are the weakest general conditions under which ignoring the process that causes missing data always leads to correct inferences."

## Role in Chapter 4

One claim, and it is exactly the one Chapter 4 needs:

> Whether the process that causes missing data can be ignored **depends on why the data are missing**, and the conditions under which it can be ignored are restrictive.

That is the whole Chapter 4 lesson about missingness at core depth. The reader does not need the formal conditions; they need to know that "ignore it and carry on" is a substantive assumption with a name, a literature, and stated conditions — not a default.

The summary also supplies, on its own authority, the terms **missing at random**, **observed at random**, and **distinct** parameters.

## Chapter 4 use and cautions

**Do not attribute MCAR / MAR / MNAR to this paper.** The summary uses *missing at random* and *observed at random*. The now-standard three-way vocabulary — missing completely at random, missing at random, missing not at random — was consolidated later in the literature, and this source as verified does not contain it. Chapter 4 should teach the underlying question in plain language and must not present the trio as Rubin's 1976 terminology.

**Do not cite page numbers.** Only the summary was read.

**Do not teach the formal conditions.** Sampling-distribution versus direct-likelihood versus Bayesian inference, and the distinctness condition, are Chapter 8 and depth-curriculum material.

**Do not use this source for selection, censoring, or provenance.** The verified summary addresses missing data only.
