# Source note: `epa2010economic`

## Bibliographic record

U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, National Center for Environmental Economics, Office of Policy. *Guidelines for Preparing Economic Analyses*. Dated 17 December 2010, updated May 2014.

## Verification status

**Read at the glossary only, printed pp. xiii–xiv**, plus the table of contents. The document runs to roughly three hundred pages of regulatory-economics guidance and **none of the body was read.**

This is a deliberately narrow use: the glossary supplies three definitions Chapter 12 needs, stated authoritatively, and the book takes nothing else.

## Verified locators

- **p. xiii**: "**Marginal benefit.** The marginal benefit is the benefit received from an incremental increase in the consumption of a good or service. It is calculated as the increase in total benefit divided by the increase in consumption."
- **p. xiii**: "**Marginal cost.** The marginal cost is the change in total cost that results from a unit increase in output. It is calculated as the increase in total cost divided by the increase in output."
- **p. xiv**: "**Opportunity cost.** Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative to a particular activity or resource. Opportunity cost need not be assessed in monetary terms. It can be assessed in terms of anything that is of value to the person or persons doing the assessing."
- **glossary**: markets are efficient when prices "reflect their marginal costs, or when marginal benefits equal marginal costs."

## Role in Chapter 12

- supplies authoritative definitions of marginal benefit and marginal cost, so the chapter's marginal reasoning is not asserted;
- supplies opportunity cost, and specifically the clause that it "need not be assessed in monetary terms" — which matters because Chapter 11's value-of-information machinery **did** require a single currency, and the chapter notes the contrast;
- supplies the efficiency condition that §5 of the manuscript then shows failing on lumpy investments.

## Cautions

**The body was not read.** No claim anywhere in this book concerns EPA regulatory practice, benefit-cost methodology, discounting, or valuation.

**§6.2.4 uses `shadow price` in a different sense** — the shadow price of capital, a discounting concept — visible in the table of contents. **That section was not read and is not characterised.** Chapter 12 uses only the optimization sense, from `boyd2004convex`, and the canon entry records that the two senses exist.

**Do not treat a glossary definition as a full treatment.** These are one-sentence definitions from a guidance document, used for their precision, not as an exposition of microeconomics.
