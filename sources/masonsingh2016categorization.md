## Role in Chapter 16

This source exists in the chapter to stop it building a method on a single 1981 finding without checking on it — which is what Chapter 9 spent a chapter warning against.

It does two things: it records **what the classic study actually consisted of**, and it reports a result that **contrasts with it**.

## Verified locators

- **Introduction**: "Categorizing or grouping together problems based upon similarity of solution is often claimed to be a hallmark of expertise"
- **Introduction**, on the classic study's size: "In the classic study conducted by Chi, Feltovich and Glaser [1] (here called the Chi study), eight introductory physics students in calculus-based [courses]" — **eight**.
- **Introduction**: "Due to the small sample size in the Chi study, we refrain from performing statistical analysis of the Chi data"
- **Introduction**, on the limits of the comparison: "Although full comparison is impossible without access to all of the original problems in the Chi study"
- **Abstract**: "Our findings, which contrast from those of Chi et al., suggest that there is a much wider distribution of expertise among introductory students."
- **Conclusions**: "the percentage of introductory physics students who chose 'surface-feature' categories such as 'ramp' and 'pulley' was significantly lower than the percentages reported in the Chi study"
- **Conclusions**: "What is more surprising however is that none of the 8 introductory students in the Chi study... chose Chi's expert categories, e.g., Newton's second law, energy principles, circular motion or linear kinematics as categories at all." Substantial minorities of the replicating study's students chose exactly those categories.
- **Conclusions**: "Our finding suggests that since expertise plays a role in categorization, it is not appropriate to call all introductory students novices as in the Chi study."

## Cautions

- **A conference-proceedings paper, short, with no printed pagination.**
- **Physics education research**, and the book applies its distinction to a different kind of problem. The widening is the book's own.
- **Do not use this source to claim the Chi finding is wrong.** It reports a different distribution in a different population, and the authors attribute the difference partly to how the two samples were recruited — in-class here, volunteer there.
- **The authors state the limits of their own comparison**, and the manuscript carries that: full comparison was impossible without the original problems.
- **What it changes is the population claim, not the distinction.** Surface and structure remain two bases for sorting; "novices sort by surface features" does not survive as a fact about beginners.
- **Neither this source nor `chi1993classic` may be used without the other.**
