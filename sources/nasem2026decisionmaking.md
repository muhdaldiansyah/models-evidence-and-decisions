# Source Note: nasem2026decisionmaking

## Citation

National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. 2026. *Advancing the Art and Science of Decision-Making: A Guide*. Washington, DC: The National Academies Press. DOI: 10.17226/29265.

## Verification

Verified against the National Academies Press publication record and the official online text.

Verified locators inspected:
- publication information / front matter for title, institutional author, year, publisher, and DOI;
- “What Makes a Good Decision?” for the distinction between decision process and outcome and the guide's factual/value competence framing;
- “Values and Ethics” for the role of values in decision-making;
- “Common Steps for Decision-Making” for the explicit separation of clarifying the decision, involving affected people, establishing facts, defining values, developing alternatives, assessing predicted consequences and uncertainty, and deciding.

## Role in Chapter 1

This source supports a practical separation among:
- the decision being made;
- factual evidence and analysis;
- values and evaluative premises;
- candidate alternatives;
- predicted consequences and uncertainty;
- the eventual choice.

It also supports treating decision framing as iterative rather than as a rigid one-directional pipeline: alternatives, consequences, uncertainty, and the participation of affected people can change how the decision is framed.

Chapter 1 uses this evidence only to establish the first-pass boundary that evidence can improve beliefs about consequences but does not by itself determine which consequences should matter most or which action should be chosen.

## Cautions

- The guide is written for transportation-agency decision-makers and is used here as an authoritative practical synthesis, not as a universal formal theory of decision-making.
- Chapter 1 does not adopt the guide's steps as a mandatory checklist or named framework.
- Formal value structuring, utility, trade-off analysis, expected utility, value of information, and decision-analysis machinery remain Chapters 10–11.
