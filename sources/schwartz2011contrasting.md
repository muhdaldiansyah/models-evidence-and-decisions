# Source Note: schwartz2011contrasting

## Citation

Daniel L. Schwartz, Catherine C. Chase, Marily A. Oppezzo, and Doris B. Chin. 2011. “Practicing Versus Inventing With Contrasting Cases: The Effects of Telling First on Learning and Transfer.” *Journal of Educational Psychology* 103(4): 759–775. DOI: 10.1037/a0025140.

## Verification

Verified against an author-provided abstract in ERIC and Stanford research pages.

## Role in Chapter 1

Support for using deliberately structured contrasts that make underlying distinctions visible instead of merely adding surface variety.

The verified abstract reports that tell-and-practice and invent-with-contrasting-cases conditions achieved similar routine procedural proficiency, while the contrasting-cases condition showed stronger learning of deep structure and more transfer to semantically different topics sharing that structure.

Chapter 1 uses the bounded implication that contrasts should isolate meaningful changes in intended use, claim type, environment properties, representation, or evidence/choice structure.

## Cautions

- Chapter 1 does not adopt the full invention-before-telling intervention used in the study.
- Do not claim that simply showing multiple examples produces deep transfer.
- Contrast quality matters more than example count; additional cases should be removed if they do not expose a distinct reasoning relation.
