## Role in Chapter 2

- supplies a source-grounded distinction between **abstraction** and **idealization**;
- supports the claim that whether a simplification is defensible depends on the modeller's goal, not on detail alone;
- supplies the multiple-models position, which grounds Chapter 2's refusal of a single "one true model";
- supports the lesson that two modellers can build the *same* model for different reasons, and that the reason determines what should happen next.

## Chapter 2 use and cautions

Chapter 2 uses the **omission versus distortion** contrast and the purpose-dependence of justification. It does not teach the three-kinds taxonomy as required reader vocabulary.

**The omission/distortion distinction is Jones's, reported approvingly by Weisberg in a footnote — it is not Weisberg's own thesis.** Cite it accordingly, or cite Jones 2005 directly after verifying that source. Weisberg himself argues for pluralism about idealization and says he sees "no reason why we should not treat minimalist modeling as a form of idealization."

Do not present the three kinds as an exhaustive or settled classification; the paper's own framing is that consensus has "clustered around" three positions.

Do not use this source for claims about aggregation, grain, or spatial scale. It does not address them.
