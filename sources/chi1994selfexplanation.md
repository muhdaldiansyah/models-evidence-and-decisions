# Source Note: chi1994selfexplanation

## Citation

Michelene T. H. Chi, Nicholas De Leeuw, Mei-Hung Chiu, and Christian Lavancher. 1994. “Eliciting Self-Explanations Improves Understanding.” *Cognitive Science* 18(3): 439–477. DOI: 10.1016/0364-0213(94)90016-7.

## Verification

Verified against an institutional publication record carrying the original article metadata and abstract.

## Role in Chapter 1

Load-bearing support for prompted self-explanation during worked-example study.

The verified abstract reports that learners prompted to self-explain gained more than rereading controls and that stronger self-explanation was associated with deeper understanding on complex inference questions and mental-model measures.

Chapter 1 uses the narrow implication that self-explanation prompts should require learners to explain why a reasoning move is appropriate, what distinction it expresses, or why a competing formulation fails.

## Cautions

- Do not treat any paraphrase prompt as equivalent to substantive self-explanation.
- Do not claim that the study validates the exact number or placement of Chapter 1 prompts.
- The experiment concerns a specific learning domain; transfer to this book's interdisciplinary reasoning task is an evidence-informed pedagogical synthesis.
