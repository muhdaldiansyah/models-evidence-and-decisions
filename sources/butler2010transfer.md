# Source Note: butler2010transfer

## Citation

Andrew C. Butler. 2010. “Repeated Testing Produces Superior Transfer of Learning Relative to Repeated Studying.” *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition* 36(5): 1118–1133. DOI: 10.1037/a0019902.

## Verification

Verified against an institutional publication record carrying the article metadata and original abstract.

## Role in Chapter 1

Load-bearing support for retrieval practice as more than a memorization exercise: the verified abstract reports four experiments in which repeated testing improved retention and performance on new inference questions, including transfer across knowledge domains, relative to repeated studying on a one-week final test.

Chapter 1 uses the bounded implication that retrieval and delayed transfer assessment are preferable to rereading alone when the goal is usable reasoning beyond the original example.

## Cautions

- Do not claim that this experiment demonstrates far transfer for the book's full first-pass reasoning competence.
- The study materials and tasks are narrower than this book's ill-structured interdisciplinary transfer forms.
- A failed cold-transfer assessment is diagnostic evidence; it should not be interpreted as proof of zero learning.
