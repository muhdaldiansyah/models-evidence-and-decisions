## Role in Chapter 17

This source is used for **exactly two things**: the attribution of statistical process control to Shewhart, and the statement that the method rests on the common-cause / special-cause distinction.

`README.md`'s Chapter 17 block permits "concept-level monitoring machinery... where appropriate", and `../decisions/0024` clause 2 records the decision to take the **distinction** and refuse the **technique**. **Nothing about control charts is taken from this source.**

## Not the primary source

The distinction originates with **Shewhart (1931), *Economic Control of Quality of Manufactured Product***, confirmed from this review's own attribution. **That book was not obtained**, so the distinction is used **as reported at** this review — the device this book has used since Chapter 6.

## Cautions

- **A literature review of healthcare applications**, which is its actual subject and which the book does not use. Read at one page of twenty-one.
- **Not the primary source.** Shewhart was not obtained.
- **Extraction hazard.** The typesetting renders `fi` as a ligature that extracts as a single character — *firstly* as *ﬁrstly*, *defined* as *deﬁned*. **No quotation taken from this source contains an `fi`.**
- **No chart machinery is taken**: no centre lines, control limits, run rules, chart types, or sampling plans.
- **The consequence the chapter draws is the book's own**, not the source's: that a threshold set inside the range of ordinary variation is a timer rather than a trigger. The source supports the distinction; the application to signposts is derived in Chapter 17 and stated as derived.
- Do not use this source for any claim about healthcare, quality improvement, or manufacturing.
